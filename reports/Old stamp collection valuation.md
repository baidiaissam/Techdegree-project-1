# Long shots hide among 140 cheap stamps

No stamp in this collection is valuable as it appears in the photos. The roughly 142 stamps are mass-printed definitives and commemoratives from 1922 to 2001, nearly all from after 1945, each worth from a cent to under a dollar, and the whole lot — including a 1983 Cyprus envelope that is worth more kept intact than taken apart — would realistically bring **about US$20–55** sold carefully as country lots, and less from a dealer buying everything at once. What the photos cannot show is whether a handful of stamps belong to scarce catalogued varieties that look identical to the naked eye: a 1962 French rooster stamp printed on trial fluorescent paper (€15–26 used at dealers), three Canadian stamps that could lack their phosphor tagging or sit on blue-tinted paper ($16.55–57.50 used in a price guide), German 1946–48 colour shades (€22.50–57.50 with an expert's certificate), and one true long shot, a 1945 AM Post 10 Pf that would be a rarity offered at **about €1,200** if its perforations gauge 11½ on all four sides. An evening with a UV torch, a perforation gauge, a millimetre ruler and a look at the backs settles most of these, and the photos already argue against several others, including the Saudi Kaaba stamps, the Canadian 2c Admiral and 80c caribou, and the US Honeybee pair. eBay blocked automated access to its sold listings for every researcher, so the eBay evidence here is current asking prices plus three recorded sales of used singles at €1.50, US$0.99 and €1.00; it matches the catalogue picture, with ordinary used singles offered at €1–2 and high asking prices confined to certified varieties or optimistic "rare" titles that show no sign of selling. The sections below rank the long shots, give a home test and a ready-made eBay "Sold items" search for each, report what eBay did and did not show, total the collection and identify every stamp; euro amounts convert at an assumed €1 ≈ US$1.15, a rate the researchers did not verify.

**The AM Post 10 Pf is the only stamp with a four-figure outcome, and the odds are long.** The 1945 Allied military-government stamps were printed in Washington, London and Brunswick; London never printed a 10 Pf ([Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol1/html/bizone.htm)), and Brunswick perforated its sheets at 11 or 11½ holes per 2 cm in four combinations ([VPS 1894](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html)). The combination with 11½ on every side, Michel 22 D, is a recognised rarity: a 2008 study counted fewer than a hundred genuinely used copies ([VPS 1894](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html)), a mint copy retails at €239 ([phila-versand](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/am-michel-nr-22-d-postfrisch/s21870)), and a used copy with an expert's report is listed on eBay at €1,197.60, though no sale at that level surfaced ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/117521292840)). The owner's copy is fresh, strongly coloured and loose, with intact perforations whose count in the photo fits a gauge of 11–11½, so the photo cannot exclude it. The ordinary Brunswick 22 A retails at €0.90 ([phila-versand](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/10-pfg-am-michel-nr-22-a-gestempelt/bz72)) and a Washington single is offered at €1.90 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/205432302563)). Forged perforations exist on this issue ([ArGe AM-Post](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)), so a positive reading means a certificate before any sale, not an instant windfall.

Smaller premiums of €7–9.50 apply to shades of the 16 Pf, 20 Pf and 50 Pf numeral ([949 b](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/alliierte-besetzung/16-pfg-arbeiter-1947-michel-nr-949-b-gestempelt/ge141); [924 b](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/alliierte-besetzung/ziffer-1946-michel-nr-924-b-gestempelt/ge64); [932 c](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/alliierte-besetzung/50-pfg-ziffer-1946-michel-nr-932-c-gestempelt/ge58)). The AM Post 3 Pf is worth €8–35 if it gauges 14, the London printing, and the 4 Pf about €21 if it gauges 11 on top and bottom and 11½ on the sides ([10 A y](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/am-michel-nr-10-a-y-gestempelt/s23744); [10 G](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/ziffer-1945-michel-nr-10-g-gestempelt/s15768); [18 B](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/am-michel-nr-18-b-gestempelt/s17236)). Every premium-priced German listing the researchers found carries an expert's signature, report or certificate, while uncertified copies of scarcer numbers sell as ordinary stamps: Washington-printed AM Post singles are offered at €1.90 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/197225888625)). The owner's 3 Pf is heavily toned and not the scarce blue-violet shade, which would sharply discount even a good variety.

**Three Canadian stamps could be errors worth 100 to 400 times a normal copy.** PSG lists the 1977 12c Angel choir without its phosphor tagging — the invisible coating that glows under UV so sorting machines can face letters — at **$49.65 used**, the 1983 32c Maple Leaf at $22.05 untagged or $16.55 with a comet-shaped tagging flaw, and the 1985 34c Queen Elizabeth II on paper tinted by an improperly wiped printing plate at **$57.50 used**, against $0.11–0.15 for normal copies ([12c](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/29542/angel-choir-untagged-error-12-cent-1977-canada-postage-stamp-christmas-jesous-ahatonhia); [32c](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/29687/maple-leaf-untagged-error-32-cent-1983-canada-postage-stamp-maple-leaf); [comet](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/29684/maple-leaf-comet-tag-flaw-32-cent-1983-canada-postage-stamp-maple-leaf); [34c](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/29695/queen-elizabeth-ii-blue-paper-improper-wiping-34-cent-1985-canada-postage-stamp-queen-elizabeth-definitives)). A confirmed error should be expertised before sale, as with the Greene Foundation certificate on a Canadian missing-colour error sold at Spink ([Spink](https://www.spink.com/lot/90371168)).

**The French rooster is the best bet, because nothing argues against it.** The 0,25 Coq de Decaris was printed about 1.5 billion times ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/3034/1962-sans-legende-particuliere-coq-de-decaris)), but in 1962 La Poste tested envelope-facing machines with two trial printings on fluorescent paper, sold them over post-office counters without announcement, and most went out on mail and were thrown away; a specialist magazine adds that undetected copies probably still sit in collections ([Delcampe Magazine](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0240/8591/files/delcampe-magazine-3-24-30.pdf); [phil-ouest](https://www.phil-ouest.com/Timbre.php?Nom_timbre=Coq_Decaris_1331N)). A coil copy with a red or green control number on the back is a smaller prize at about €7 used ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/5920/1962-sans-legende-particuliere-coq-de-decaris)). Fluorescence never shows in a normal photograph, so only a UV lamp can rule it out.

## One evening with four cheap tools settles most questions

The checks need a long-wave (365 nm) ultraviolet torch — a banknote checker works for paper fluorescence, while a collector's lamp with a short-wave setting shows Canadian tagging best — plus a perforation gauge, a 10× loupe, a millimetre ruler, daylight and stamp tongs. Everything except reading the back can be done with stamps left on their pages, and nothing should be soaked, cleaned or rubbed: glued stamps risk thin spots and running felt-pen ink for gains of cents. Never look into a short-wave lamp, and work in short bursts. If a stamp tests positive, leave it as it is, photograph front and back, and ask for a fee quote before paying for a certificate; no fee schedule was found, and fees could exceed the gain on anything worth under about €20. The relevant experts appear throughout the evidence: BPP examiners Andreas Schlegel and Andreas Wehner for AM Post ([ArGe AM-Post](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)), the Kontrollrat working group for German colour shades ([phila-versand](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/alliierte-besetzung/ziffer-1946-michel-nr-934-b-gestempelt/s24554)), the Greene Foundation for Canada ([Spink](https://www.spink.com/lot/90371168)), the Philatelic Foundation for US errors ([Rumsey](https://rumseyauctions.com/auctions/lot/26/2017)), Italian periti such as Diena for Castelli varieties ([eBay.it](https://www.ebay.it/b/Francobolli-castelli-rari-a-francobolli-della-repubblica-italiana-dal-1965-al-1985/36485/bn_7005874706)) and French experts such as Calvès, whose signature accompanied an auctioned fluorescent Coq ([Interencheres](https://www.interencheres.com/en-US/art-decoration/vente-chrono-de-timbre-poste-682662/lot-87494115.html)). A German collectors' guide suggests expertising AM Post stamps only when they are catalogued above about €5 ([VPS 1894](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-1-die-basissammlung.html)).

### A UV torch screens most candidates in one dark room

A glow only means something relative to a normal stamp, so darken the room and compare each candidate with its neighbours. Shine the torch on the Coq de Decaris beside any other 1960s French stamp: if the whole paper, margins and perforation teeth included, glows clearly **bright yellow** — pale yellow for type I, golden for type II — while the other stays dull, it is a 1331d candidate ([phil-ouest](https://www.phil-ouest.com/Timbre.php?Nom_timbre=Coq_Decaris_1331C); [Delcampe Magazine](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0240/8591/files/delcampe-magazine-3-24-30.pdf)); a bluish-white glow usually comes from washing-powder brighteners, not trial paper. Then light the Canadian 12c Angel choir and 32c Maple Leaf alongside the 8c skating, 32c Air Force and 64c Christmas stamps on the same card: normal general tagging glows "brilliant yellow, faintly tinged with green" with no afterglow ([adminware](https://adminware.ca/checklist/chk_tag.htm)), so a 12c or 32c that **stays dark while its neighbours glow** is a possible untagged error, and a stray comet-shaped streak on the 32c is the comet flaw. Next, light all nineteen Italian castles at once: most should react identically, so set aside any 100 L that stays dull or any 300 L that glows differently from the rest ([Il Postalista VII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/033.pdf)). Each French Liberté 0,20 should show **one glowing bar along its right margin**; no bar, half bars, a central bar or two bars is a variety worth €0.50–2 used ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/825/1982-sans-legende-particuliere-type-liberte-de-gandon); [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/310914581092)). Three curiosities can share the session: the two West German 50 Pf earth-station stamps, issued on two paper types that specialists separate by fluorescence but with no price data ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrie_und_Technik)); the Canadian 1c Wilding, where glowing bands at the side edges plus a brief afterglow mean Winnipeg tagging worth $0.92 used ([adminware](https://adminware.ca/checklist/chk_tag.htm); [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28611/queen-elizabeth-ii-violet-brown-w2b-1-cent-1962-canada-postage-stamp)); and the German shade candidates, some of which "differ clearly under UV light" according to collectors ([philaforum](https://philaforum.com/forum/thread/9495-am-post/)).

### A perforation gauge separates printings the eye cannot

Lay the stamp on the gauge and slide it until the printed dots sit in every hole along one edge, then read all four edges separately; German catalogues quote the top-and-bottom gauge first. For the AM Post 10 Pf, start with the loupe on the ornament just left of the "P" of PFENNIG: a **white triangle** there means the Brunswick printing, and its absence means Washington, which was perforated 11 only and cannot be a 22 D ([Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol1/html/bizone.htm); [VPS 1894](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html)). Then gauge it: 11 all round is the common 22 A (€0.90); **11½ on all four edges is a possible 22 D** that should go untouched, unsoaked and unpressed to a BPP AM Post examiner; and 14 on any edge is suspicious, because London printed no 10 Pf and forgers produced perforations that were never issued ([ArGe AM-Post](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)). On the 4 Pf, 11 at top and bottom with 11½ on the sides is the 18 B (about €21 signed), 11½ at top and bottom or all round gives 18 C or 18 D (€4–5), 11 all round is 18 A (€0.50) and about 14 is the London 11 C (about €2) ([18 B](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/am-michel-nr-18-b-gestempelt/s17236); [18 C](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/4-pfg-am-michel-nr-18-c-gestempelt/bz56); [11 C](https://www.phila-versand.de/de/briefmarken/deutsche-gebiete/bizone-spezial/4-pfg-am-michel-nr-11-c-gestempelt/bz19)). On the 3 Pf, about 14 on every edge means London (€8–35 depending on the exact perforation letter), confirmed by a fine dot screen under the loupe, since only London used photogravure ([ArGe AM-Post](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)); at 11 or 11½ only a clearly blue-violet copy is worth pursuing. The Italian 1000 L reveals a forgery the same way: the genuine 1980 Castelli are comb-perforated about 14¼ × 13¼ (as listed for the 600 L), while the 1000 L postal forgeries measure 13 (Milan) or about 12 (Verzuolo) with coarse printing and no real watermark; perforation alone is not proof, because a forged 350 L measured 14 × 13¼ ([allnumis](https://allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/italy/castle-fortress/600-lire-1980-castello-sacligero-sirmione-12084); [Il Postalista VIII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/037.pdf); [Il Foglio 190](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/027.pdf)). While the loupe is out, check that the 1000 L shows its "1", all three zeros and both I's of ITALIA. Three lower-stakes readings finish the session: the Saudi Kaaba stamps gauge about 14 for the 1982 printing and 12 for the 1984–87 reprints, with no premium found for either ([TouchStamps](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/1236766/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca)); the Moroccan Msouffa gauges 15 as a sheet stamp and 13 × 12¾ from booklets, with no sales of the booklet version found ([TouchStamps](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/339392/msouffa)); and the Canadian 80c caribou's 14½ × 14 perforation (Scott 1180c) is priced at US$3 used by one dealer, against $0.50 for the others in PSG ([Vista Stamps](https://www.arpinphilately.com/itm/canada-stamp-1180c-peary-caribou-80-1990)). Off-centre or ragged perforations are common on these issues and earn no premium ([philaforum](https://philaforum.com/forum/thread/9495-am-post/)).

### A ruler, the stamp's back and daylight finish the job

Measure the Saudi Kaaba stamps' printed picture, not the perforations: **about 26 × 21 mm is an ordinary sheet stamp worth pennies; about 29 × 19 mm is a 1986 booklet stamp** catalogued near US$9–11 ([TouchStamps sheet](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/346444/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca); [TouchStamps booklet](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/1596948/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca)). A ruler also separates AM Post printings, since the Washington design is about 4 mm wider than Brunswick's, best judged beside a cheap reference copy ([VPS 1894](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-1-die-basissammlung.html)). Backs can be read only on loose or hinged stamps; never peel a glued one. Look for a small red or green control number on the back of the Coq, which marks a coil stamp worth about €7 used ([phil-ouest](https://www.phil-ouest.com/Timbre.php?Nom_timbre=Coq_Decaris_1331C)); on the loose 70 Pf accident-prevention stamp in photo 3, where every fifth coil stamp carries a black, red, green or blue number, worth €0.50–5 mint at a dealer and offered at about €1 used ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfallverh%C3%BCtung_%28Briefmarkenserie%29); [Philmaster](https://www.philmaster-shop.de/kategorie/339/briefmarken-deutschland/deutschlandbund/rollenmarken/unfall-schwarze-nr); [eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/313049075876)); and on the loose 20 Pf Lorsch stamp and the loose Dutch numeral in photo 1, both from coil printings ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bauwerke_aus_zw%C3%B6lf_Jahrhunderten); [allnumis](https://www.allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/netherlands/numerals-figures/65-cent-1986-10480)). The back also settles two descriptions: full gum on the unpostmarked Senegal Pottery 30F means it is unused, and full gum under a neat corner cancel on the Guinea 150F means it was cancelled to order rather than posted, worth cents either way ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancelled-to-order)). In daylight, lay the Canadian 34c on plain white paper next to another 1980s Canadian stamp and look for an **even blue tint across the whole paper, border included** ([PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/29695/queen-elizabeth-ii-blue-paper-improper-wiping-34-cent-1985-canada-postage-stamp-queen-elizabeth-definitives)). Compare each German shade candidate with a common copy of the same value bought for cents, and pursue only a distinctly deeper or brighter tone. Check the castles for a grey 600 L with no green, a 650 L missing yellow or black, or a 450 L printed only in slate ([Il Postalista VII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/033.pdf); [Il Foglio 190](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/027.pdf)); a "blue castle" 1000 L deserves suspicion, since used copies are often faked by fading, which turns the meadow turquoise while the screen dots of the "missing" colour still show under a loupe ([Il Postalista VIII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/037.pdf)). Finally, read SOCIALE on the Italian Social Republic 20c ([italianstamps.co.uk](https://italianstamps.co.uk/rsi)), look for a tiny "1" or "2" plate number in the margin or the design of the Honeybee pair ([Mystic Stamp](https://www.mysticstamp.com/Products/United-States/2281/USA/)), and check the top and bottom edges of the West German 10–40 Pf accident-prevention stamps, where one straight, unperforated edge marks a booklet stamp worth about €1–2 used ([Leuchtturm](https://www.leuchtturm.de/media/cms/files/t9829g095615einzelblattverzeichnis_ref_nr_23AZ.pdf); [eBay sold](https://www.ebay.de/itm/117447123065); [eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/315134132579)).

## eBay hid its sold prices, but its asking prices agree with the catalogues

The owner asked specifically for eBay prices, and all six research streams tried to pull them on 15 November 2026. eBay returned HTTP 403 error pages to automated requests for search pages, the "Sold items" filter and individual listings on ebay.com, .de, .ca, .fr, .it and .nl ([attempted Sold search](https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw=canada+2c+green+admiral+used&_sacat=260&LH_Sold=1&LH_Complete=1&_ipg=60)); the sold-price archives WorthPoint and 130point were blocked as well ([WorthPoint](https://www.worthpoint.com/inventory/search?query=honeybee+2281+error&sort=SaleDate); [130point](https://back.130point.com/sales/)); and PicClick, which mirrors eBay, shows only live listings because its sold filter is disabled in its page code ([PicClick](https://picclick.de/?q=AM+Post+22+D)). **No researcher obtained eBay sold data in any useful quantity.** The eBay evidence in this report is therefore current asking prices, captured through PicClick for the German material and through search-engine snapshots for everything else, plus three sales that surfaced in those snapshots; for the Moroccan and Arab stamps, where eBay showed almost nothing, completed sales on Delcampe, a large European stamp marketplace, stand in.

**The three recorded sales tell the story on their own.** A used London-printed AM Post 4 Pf sold for €1.50 in July 2025 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/197260640902)), a used 1953 Dutch Juliana 45c — one of the owner's own stamps — sold for US$0.99 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/266029014331)), and a used West Berlin accident-prevention booklet stamp sold for €1.00 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/117447123065)). Asking prices for ordinary used singles cluster at the same level: €1.90 for AM Post singles ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/205432302563)), €1.00 for a numbered West German coil stamp ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/313049075876)), US$1.99 for a used Castelli ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/297014039366)) and about US$1.10 for a used 80c caribou ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/206125587817)). Those are sellers' minimum listing prices, which buy postage and often go unsold, not values. Bulk listings show what this kind of material really fetches: a 75-stamp Senegal lot asked at US$6.97, about nine cents a stamp ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/146672482165)); 110 different 1970s–90s Canadian stamps at about C$6 and a 42-stamp 1980s lot at about C$1 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.ca/b/Used-Canadian-Stamp-Collections-Lots/3486/bn_96158926)); and a used Senegal collection with a US$145 catalogue value offered at about US$42, or 29% of catalogue ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/194176321921)).

**High eBay prices belong to certified varieties or hopeful titles, and many do not sell.** The high asks include €220–287.50 for certified AM Post 3 Pf 17 b D copies ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/365926017964); [eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/377605994868)), €1,197.60 for the certified 22 D ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/117521292840)), €375–449.95 for mint fluorescent Coqs ([eBay](https://www.ebay.fr/itm/FRANCE-STAMP-TIMBRE-1331d-COQ-DECARIS-25c-PAPIER-FLUORESCENT-NEUFxxLUXE-T277A-/392109599667)) and up to €2,750 for a major Castelli 100 L error ([eBay.it](https://www.ebay.it/sch/i.html?_sacat=7662&_nkw=variet%C3%A0%20castelli)). Signs of thin demand are everywhere: an AM Post plate-flaw stamp with a stated catalogue value of €350 sat at €25 with no bids ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/198795084100)), a London 3 Pf auction starting at €15 drew no bids ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/227608084171)), a cover with two stamps described as catalogued at about €250 sat at €65 with no bids ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/327457673387)), and sellers quote "Michel 75" and "Michel 140" for used Control Council sets they ask €14.99 and €27.99 for ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/136705031137); [eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/137505525389)). Where real results exist they land well below catalogue: a Honeybee black-omitted error catalogued by Scott at $500 sold for $250, and mint imperforate Honeybee pairs estimated at $100–150 sold for $55 ([StampAuctionNetwork](https://stampauctionnetwork.com/sr/sr10429.cfm); [Rumsey](https://rumseyauctions.com/auctions/lot/26/2012)). Delcampe's completed sales of the owner's Moroccan stamps run at roughly 10–50% of the Yvert values sellers quote ([Delcampe](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/stamps/morocco-1956/maroc-n-536-oblitere-cote-0-20eur-roi-hassan-ii-955258031.html)). The clearest warning is the ordinary Castelli 100 L titled "RARO" and asked at €1–20, once €305, with no evidence that any sold ([eBay.it](https://www.ebay.it/itm/FRANCOBOLLO-100-LIRE-CASTELLO-ARAGONESE-ISCHIA-RARO-/254800262801); [eBay.it](https://www.ebay.it/itm/402669294276)): on eBay, a title or a quoted catalogue value is not a price, and only the Sold filter shows what buyers paid.

## The whole collection is worth roughly US$20–55

Adding up the researchers' group estimates, which rest on dealer prices, price guides and the sold and asking prices above, gives **about US$20–55 for the 142 stamps and the Cyprus envelope** sold carefully as country lots, with the envelope alone making up about a quarter of that range. A dealer buying everything in one go would pay a fraction of it: one dealer-affiliated German guide, seen only in search excerpts, puts typical buying offers for standard post-war West German material under 6% of Michel catalogue ([Briefmarkenmesse Essen](https://www.briefmarkenmesse-essen.de/verkaufen/wert/)). **Condition drags the low end down, and the upside is real but bounded.** Two album pages are glued and marked with felt-pen ink, one Heinemann stamp is defaced by ink across the portrait, and the toned AM Post 3 Pf, the creased 32c Maple Leaf, the damaged Canadian Air Force and Moroccan energy stamps and a torn Dutch railway stamp would all be discounted. Each mid-range variety confirmed by the home checks would add roughly **US$10–65 at retail** before an expert's fee, so even two or three hits would double or triple the total rather than transform it; only a certified AM Post 22 D would move the collection into four figures. Selling single stamps costs more in fees and postage than most of them are worth, so country lots, or keeping the collection whole, make more sense, and pairs, stamps on piece and the Cyprus envelope should stay intact because separating them destroys what little premium they carry. ### Morocco and the Arab world: Delcampe sales of 3 to 45 cents

Sixteen Moroccan stamps, one each from Algeria and Tunisia, two from Libya and four from Saudi Arabia, all postally used issues of 1968–2001. eBay yielded no sold data and no prices for these exact stamps, only titles of related sets, so the price evidence comes from Delcampe's completed sales, which put used singles of these stamps at **US$0.03–0.45**, roughly 10–50% of the €0.15–0.75 Yvert values sellers quote ([Delcampe](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/stamps/morocco-1956/maroc-n-536-oblitere-cote-0-20eur-roi-hassan-ii-955258031.html); [Delcampe](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/stamps/philately-coins/coins/maroc-poste-obl-yv-749-mi-827-monnaie-frappee-a-sabta-cachet-rond-2264384866.html)). ## Conclusion

The value question for this collection has turned from a research problem into a measurement problem. Every remaining uncertainty — fluorescent paper, missing tagging, a gauge reading, a tint, a number on the back — is a physical property that photographs do not record, so more searching online cannot move the estimate, while an evening with a UV torch and a gauge can. The evidence from five national markets also converges on one rule that should govern the next step: real sales land at roughly 10–50% of catalogue, and an expert's certificate, not a catalogue number or an eBay title, is what turns a variety into money. The sequence is therefore short: test first; run the Sold search and ask for a fee quote only for a stamp that tests positive and is catalogued above about €20; and sell everything else as country lots, or keep it.

Two pointers reach beyond these photos. If the bag or other album pages hold stamps the photos do not show, two kinds deserve their own check: West German stamps inscribed "Deutsche Bundespost Berlin", printed in far smaller numbers than the versions here ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfallverh%C3%BCtung_%28Briefmarkenserie%29)), and pre-1976 Saudi definitives priced in piastres, whose printing varieties fell outside this research. And the Cyprus envelope, the one item whose value comes from its context rather than a hidden flaw, carries the general lesson for a family collection like this one: keeping pairs, pieces and covers together preserves more value than taking them apart.
