Scope: 13 Canadian stamps and 1 US pair (2 stamps) in photos 2, 3, 8 and 10. All are used. I examined zoomed crops of every stamp.

## 1. What exactly is each stamp (Scott no.), what is its used value, and what does it realistically sell for?

### Takeaway
Every stamp in this group is a common, normal stamp. As an ordinary used copy, each is worth well under US$1. Market averages for used very fine copies run from about $0.11 to $0.57. Several preliminary identifications needed correcting. Most importantly, the 3c red is the 1932 Medallion issue. The 12c is the "Angel choir" design, the 64c is from 1983, and the Air Force and Trefflé Berthiaume stamps are from 1984.

### Inferences
- The realistic selling price is far below the PSG figures. PSG averages reflect dealer and auction listings for sound copies. These copies have heavy cancels, are glued to cardboard, or have faults (torn corner on the Air Force stamp, damaged edges on the 1c, toning and a crease on the 32c Leaf). Realistically each stamp sells for a few cents, and the whole group as normal copies might fetch about US$1–3 from a collector or dealer. This is my estimate. It is supported by eBay asking prices for bulk lots of 50–110 different used Canadian stamps at a few dollars per lot (see Q6). I found no sold-price data.
- The PSG used values add up to about $3.12 for all 15 stamps: 0.24 + 0.13 + 0.12 + 0.12 + 0.14 + 0.13 + 0.15 + 0.57 + 0.14 + 0.15 + 0.50 + 0.14 + 0.11 + 0.48.
- Do not peel or soak the stamps off the card. For stamps worth cents, removing them risks damage for no gain. The UV and perforation checks in Q5 can be done with the stamps left in place.

## 2. King George V 2c green Admiral and 3c red: which varieties are valuable, how can the owner check, and what are they worth used?

### Takeaway
**2c green Admiral:** a used copy is only worth more than US$10 if it is one of two formats:
- the perf 12 horizontal "endwise" coil, Scott 133 (PSG U-VF $55, U-F $27.55);
- the 1924 imperforate, Scott 137 (PSG U-VF $27.55).

Everything else is cheap used:
- wet vs dry printing and shades: $0.18–0.24;
- perf 8 coil: $1.10–2.39;
- booklet single: $2.50;
- thin paper: $1.95.

The owner's copy appears perforated on all four sides, so it is an ordinary sheet stamp.

**1932 Medallion 3c:** Die I and Die II are worth about the same used ($0.13 vs $0.18). The booklet single is $0.74 and the coil (Scott 207) $0.37. Nothing on the 3c approaches $10 used.

### Cited Findings

#### 2c green Admiral (Scott 107)
- **Wet and dry printings.** Until December 1922 all Admirals were wet-printed: the paper was dampened before printing. Dry printing on pre-gummed paper came later. The 2c green sheet stamp, 2c green sidewise coil and 2c green booklet pane of six exist both ways — [BNAPS Van Someren, Printings](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Printing.htm)
- **How to tell wet from dry.** Wet-printed stamps shrank across the paper grain and are about half a millimetre narrower than dry-printed ones of the same height. The easy test is to lay one stamp on top of another. Use reference copies perforated on all four sides: the 2c carmine sheet stamp is always wet-printed, and the 3c carmine sheet stamp is always dry-printed — [BNAPS, Printings](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Printing.htm)
- **Used values by variety (PSG U-VF):**
  - wet yellow green (base): $0.24 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/15464/king-george-v-yellow-green-wet-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - dry green: $0.18 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28328/king-george-v-green-dry-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - dry yellow green: $0.18 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28331/king-george-v-yellow-green-dry-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - deep green wet: $0.18. The same shade is $64.50 mint never hinged, so shade matters only for mint — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28330/king-george-v-deep-green-wet-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - thin paper: $1.95 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28323/king-george-v-yellow-green-wet-printing-thin-paper-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - booklet single: $2.50 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28325/king-george-v-yellow-green-booklet-single-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
- **Coils and imperforate, used values (PSG):**
  - sidewise coil perf 8 vertical, wet: U-VF $1.10 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/27820/king-george-v-green-horizontal-imperforate-wet-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - sidewise coil perf 8 vertical, dry: U-VF $2.39 — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/28413/king-george-v-green-horizontal-imperforate-dry-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - **endwise coil perf 12 horizontal: U-VF $55.00, U-F $27.55, U-VG $16.55** — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/27825/king-george-v-yellow-green-vertical-imperforate-perf-12-wet-printing-2-cent-1922-canada-postage-stamp)
  - **imperforate (6 Oct 1924): U-VF $27.55, U-F $19.30** — [PSG](https://postagestampguide.com/canada/stamps/27828/king-george-v-green-imperforate-2-cent-1924-canada-postage-stamp)
- **Scott numbers for the formats:** 128 = 2c green perf 8 vertical coil (1922); 133 = 2c yellow green perf 12 horizontal coil (1924); 137 = 2c green imperforate (6 Oct 1924) — [stampsmarter](https://stampsmarter.org/public/Stamps_Canada.php?recperpage=ALL)
- **What the coils look like.** Endwise coils have their perforations along the top and bottom: first perf 8, later perf 12. All sidewise coils are perf 8 vertically, with perforations on the left and right. In 1924 the 1c yellow, 2c green and 3c carmine were issued in imperforate panes. These were not distributed to post offices; they were sold through the Philatelic Agency, and some were released by favour — [BNAPS, Formats](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Formats.htm)
- **Coil rolls were hand-made.** Rolls of 500 were made by pasting strips of 10 or 20 stamps end to end. Paste-up singles, and coil singles with a piece of selvedge attached, are fairly common — [BNAPS, Coils](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Coils.htm)
- **Straight edges do not mean a coil.** From mid-January 1914, sheets of 400 were cut into panes of 100 with no gutters, so stamps along the cuts have a straight edge on one side, and corner stamps on two. These are sheet stamps. Booklet panes also produce straight-edged singles — [BNAPS, Straight Edges](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-StraightEdge.htm)
- **No dies on the 2c green.** BNAPS lists the two-die Admiral values as the 1c yellow, 3c carmine and the 2c+1c War Tax stamps only — [BNAPS, Dies](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Dies.htm)
- **Shades.** Marler records two wet-printing shades (green, deep green) and one dry (yellow green), but most authorities recognise one wet and one dry shade. This comes from a search snippet of the RPSC Admiral site; the page itself was behind a verification wall — [RPSC Admiral 2c green](https://rpsc.org/Library/admiral/2cgrshts.htm)
- **Example re-entry.** A documented 2c green re-entry from plate 161, lower-left pane, position 69 (161LL69) shows doubled lines at the lower right: the lower-right frame line, under the numeral box, the S of CENTS and the "2". Re-entries are catalogued at the Greene Foundation archive (re-entries.greenefoundationarchive.ca) — [BNAPS, Varieties](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Varieties.htm)
- **Thin and horizontally wove paper.** Horizontally wove paper shows a horizontal grain when held up to the light. The best-known examples are early 1c green and 2c carmine booklet panes, not the 2c green — [BNAPS, Paper](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Paper.htm)
- **The owner's copy:** perforations visible along the top, both sides and the bottom right; a wavy machine cancel; glued to card. The bottom-left perforations are partly hidden by shadow and marker — [Photo 8](/root/.claude/uploads/f12d27b4-af0e-510c-8aa8-9ca20af62021/5d9bef50-image.jpg)

#### 1932 Medallion 3c deep red (Scott 197)
- **Die types.** The only well-documented die difference in the Medallion issue is on the 3c. The first two plates (Die 1) "had the top point of the 3's even with the white horizontal line above the word 'cents'". Two plates were Die 1 and ten plates were Die 2 — [Canadian Philately blog, Medallion Part 1](https://canadianphilately.blogspot.com/2016/10/the-medallion-issue-of-1932-1935-part-1.html). ### Cited Findings

#### 1c Wilding (Scott 337)
- **Winnipeg tagging.** Phosphor tagging was introduced on Canadian stamps on 13 Jan 1962, during the Wilding definitives. On the 1c, 2c, 3c and 5c, an 8 mm vertical phosphor bar was applied down the perforations, so each stamp carries a band at its edges. Tagged stamps can be identified with an ultraviolet light, and the phosphor leaves a very brief afterglow when the light is removed. Some tagged stamps show a yellowish coating on the left and right edges visible to the naked eye. Highly fluorescent paper can hide the tagging, in which case use the afterglow test — [adminware, Canadian phosphor tagging](https://adminware.ca/checklist/chk_tag.htm)

#### Flagged — worth a closer look (step-by-step for a non-expert)
These are listed by possible upside. Tools: a 10× magnifier, a perforation gauge (sold cheaply by stamp dealers), and a stamp-collector's UV lamp with both short-wave and long-wave settings. Short-wave UV is harmful to eyes and skin, so never look into the lamp and work in short bursts. The stamps can stay glued to the card for all of these checks.

1. **12c 1977 Christmas "Angel choir" (photo 8): check for missing tagging (untagged error, about $49.65 used).**
   1. In a dark room, shine the UV lamp on the face of the 12c, then on two or three other 1972–90 Canadian stamps on the same card, such as the 8c skating, 32c Air Force and 64c Christmas.
   2. Normal general-tagged stamps show tagging glowing "brilliant yellow, faintly tinged with green", with no afterglow — [adminware](https://adminware.ca/checklist/chk_tag.htm)
   3. If the 12c shows no tagging glow at all while the others do, it may be the untagged error. Set it aside for expertising before selling.
2. **32c Maple Leaf (photo 2): check for missing tagging (about $22 used) and a comet-shaped tagging flaw (about $16.55 used).**
   1. Do the same UV test as for the 12c. A stray comet-shaped streak or blob of glowing tagging is the "comet tag flaw".
   2. Then examine the edges with the magnifier out of the plastic bag:
      - one or two straight edges means a booklet single (about $0.37);
      - straight top and bottom edges with perforations only at the sides would mean the coil, Scott 951. I did not retrieve a used value for it, but nothing suggests it is worth much. The photo seems to show perforations top and bottom, so this is unlikely.
3. **34c QEII (photo 2): check for the "blue paper – improper wiping" variety (about $57.50 used).**
   1. In daylight, place the stamp next to a sheet of plain white paper and a second, ordinary 1980s Canadian stamp.
   2. If the white border and the paper of the 34c have a distinct overall blue tint (a wash of ink, not a cancel mark), it is a candidate for expertising. In the photo the border looks white or cream, so this is probably a normal copy.
4. **2c green Admiral (photo 8): confirm the format; the value only changes if it is not a normal sheet stamp.**
   1. Use the magnifier to check all four edges.
      - Perforation teeth on all four sides: an ordinary sheet stamp, $0.10–0.24. The photo suggests this is the case.
      - One straight edge: a sheet-edge or booklet stamp. Booklet singles are about $2.50 used, but the two cannot be told apart reliably without a specialist.
      - Straight edges top and bottom, with large widely spaced holes (perf 8: about 8 holes in 2 cm, measured with the gauge) on the left and right: the sidewise coil, Scott 128, $1.10–2.39.
      - Straight left and right edges with fine perf 12 holes along the top and bottom: the endwise coil, Scott 133, $16–55 used. Have it expertised, because trimmed fakes exist.
   2. Optional: hold it up to the light. Noticeably thin paper is about $1.95 used.
   3. Optional: wet or dry printing. Wet prints are about 0.5 mm narrower — [BNAPS](https://bnaps.org/ore/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps/VanSomeren-AdmiralStamps-Printing.htm). This makes no meaningful difference to the used value.
5. **3c Medallion (photo 2): optional die check (no value impact).**
   1. With the magnifier, look at whether the top point of the "3" numerals is level with the white horizontal line above "CENTS". If so, it is Die I — [Medallion blog](https://canadianphilately.blogspot.com/2016/10/the-medallion-issue-of-1932-1935-part-1.html)
   2. Both dies are worth about $0.13–0.18 used. A straight edge would mean a booklet single (about $0.74).
6. **1c Wilding (photo 8): check for Winnipeg tagging (about $0.92 used; on fluorescent paper about $2.76).**
   1. Under UV, look for glowing bands along the left and right edges.
   2. Switch the lamp off: a brief afterglow confirms phosphor tagging — [adminware](https://adminware.ca/checklist/chk_tag.htm)
   3. Low value; worth checking only for interest.
7. **80c Peary caribou (photo 8): optional perforation check.**
   1. Lay it on a perforation gauge.
      - 13 (or 13.1) all round: the base stamp.
      - 12½×13: the booklet single.
      - 14½×14 (PSG: 14.4×13.8): Scott 1180c. Its used value is $0.50 per PSG, but one dealer prices it at US$3.00.
   2. Its colours are normal, so it is not the £280 missing-colours error.
8. **Honeybee pair (photo 10): plate number check.**
   1. With a 10× magnifier, examine the white margin under each stamp, and the lower part of the design, for a tiny "1" or "2". There is a small dark mark under the right stamp.
   2. A plate number would add only a little value.
   3. Colour-omitted and imperforate errors are ruled out, because "25 USA", the yellow background, the black bee detail and the perforations are all present.

### Inferences
- **Asking prices are not the stamps' worth.** The US$1–3 asked for single ordinary used stamps mostly reflects sellers' minimum listing prices and postage. Bulk lots work out at a few cents per stamp. That is the better guide to what this group would realistically fetch, and it agrees with PSG's $0.10–0.57 averages.
- **Real money is attached only to varieties.** High eBay asking prices for these issues belong to error and variety items: Honeybee black-omitted and imperforate, Admiral coils, untagged errors. Where actual results exist, even genuine errors sold below catalogue. At Rumsey auctions, 2281c (Scott $500) sold for $250, and two mint 2281a imperforate pairs (estimate $100–150) sold for $55 (Q4). The 1c Wilding "US$12 sold" claim is exactly the kind of eBay figure that should not be relied on.
- **eBay titles can misattribute catalogue numbers.** "Fast Bee" listings are colour shifts (freaks), not the catalogued yellow-omitted error, so the seller who titled one "2281e" has misattributed it. This illustrates why eBay titles are not evidence of identity or value.

### Gaps
- **No eBay sold prices or sale dates could be retrieved for any stamp in this group.** Someone using a normal web browser can get them by searching eBay with the "Sold items" filter for queries such as "Canada 107 used", "Canada 742 untagged", "Canada 926 blue paper" or "2281c honeybee", or by using WorthPoint or eBay's Terapeak with an account.
