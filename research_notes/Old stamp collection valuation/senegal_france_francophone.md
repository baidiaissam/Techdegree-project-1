# Senegal, France, Guinea and New Caledonia stamps (mostly 1960s–1980s): identification, catalog numbers and value

Scope: the in-group stamps visible in Photos 1, 2, 3 (one plastic bag photographed three times) and Photos 10 and 11 (two album pages). I looked at every photo and zoomed in on each stamp. Photo files: Photo 1 = f0575067-image.jpg, Photo 2 = cd726839-image.jpg, Photo 3 = 96709565-image.jpg, Photo 10 = af2b7d9d-image.jpg, Photo 11 = 24cabb18-image.jpg (all in /root/.claude/uploads/f12d27b4-af0e-510c-8aa8-9ca20af62021/).

Research note on access (November 2026): Colnect (Anubis anti-bot wall), StampWorld (HTTP 403), StampData (members only), eBay (HTTP 403 for both ebay.com and ebay.fr, including sold-item searches) and Delcampe search (returns no results to scripts) could not be read directly. French stamps are fully sourced from phil-ouest.com, wikitimbres.fr and stampsmarter.org, which give Yvert, Scott and Michel numbers. For most 1985–1989 Senegal stamps, **per-stamp Yvert/Scott numbers could not be verified**. The notes give whatever set-level anchors I could source and label every gap. Any StampWorld or eBay figure below comes from a search-engine snapshot of that page, not from the page itself.

## 1. What exactly is each stamp (Yvert no., Scott no.), what is its used catalog value, and what does it realistically sell for?

### Takeaway
All 20 entries in the preliminary list (25 stamps in all, counting pairs and duplicates) are common, mass-printed stamps. The French ones have sourced numbers and price-guide values of €0.10–€0.15 used each. Senegal, Guinea and New Caledonia used singles of this period retail at about €0.20 or less, and sell for cents in lots. Several preliminary IDs needed correction. The "Argentine 3 – R.F.A. 2" stamp is confirmed as an overprint. The "Le Cerf" stamp is France 1988 Buffon 3,00 F (Yvert 2540 / Scott 2124). There are 2 Liberté 0,20 copies, not up to 4, and 2 Sommet copies, not 3.

#### Corrections to the preliminary identifications
1. **#2 Sommet de la Francophonie: 2 copies, not 3.** Photo 2 shows both. Photo 1 (top edge) shows the same two. Photo 3's copy is the right-hand copy of Photo 2: it has the same blue ink smear and heavy black cancel on the "SENEGAL" side (Photos 1–3).
2. **#17 Liberté 0,20: 2 copies, not up to 4.** The Photo 1 copy (next to the Poterie stamp) is the one at the bottom-left of Photo 2. The Photo 3 copy (cancel "…OX" above the face) is the one at the top of Photo 2. The two copies have different cancels (Photos 1–3).
3. **#20 "Le Cerf" identified:** France 1988 "Histoire naturelle de Buffon – Le cerf", **3,00 F**, Yvert 2540 / Scott 2124 / Michel 2677 ([phil-ouest](https://www.phil-ouest.com/Timbre.php?Nom_timbre=Cerf_1988); [stampsmarter](https://stampsmarter.org/public/Stamps_France.php?page=207)).
4. **#7 "Argentine 3 – R.F.A. 2" is an overprint.** It is the 1986 World Cup Mexico 125F, from a set of 125F, 135F, 165F and 340F, all overprinted "ARGENTINA 3 R.F.A. 2" ([SOTW](https://www.stampsoftheworld.co.uk/wiki/Senegal_1986_Football_World_Cup_-_Mexico)). On the stamp the overprint reads "ARGENTINE".
5. **#8 50F** is the **1985 International Youth Year** issue ("Année internationale de la jeunesse", imprint "Postes 1985"), not just "communication" (Photo 10).
6. **#4 FAO 145F** is a **World Food Day** stamp ("Journée mondiale de l'alimentation"). Its imprint year looks like 1986, but I could not read it with certainty. It was postmarked 10 Dec 1988 (Photo 10).
7. **#5 Météorologie 145F** is the **World Meteorological Day, 23 March 1988** issue (Photo 10).
8. **#6 Lutte 165F pair** is dated "Postes 1986", but StampWorld lists the wrestling set under **1987** ([search snapshot of StampWorld](https://www.stampworld.com/en/stamps/Senegal/Postage%20stamps/1887-2012?page=20)). The pair is **on a piece of envelope**.
9. **#11 Industries:** the Photo 10 train/gear stamp and the Photo 11 "Les industries au Sénégal 5F" strip are the top and bottom halves of the same design. They are **probably one physical stamp** seen across overlapping pages. The set was issued 7 Nov 1988 ([StampWorld via search](https://www.stampworld.com/en/stamps/Senegal/Postage%20stamps/?year=1988)).
10. **#14 Élégance sénégalaise 40F** is a long-running engraved **definitive** ("République du Sénégal"), not a 1980s commemorative. The series existed by 1972 ([eBay listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/335444677730)).
11. **#15 Guinea:** confirmed as the Seoul 1988 Olympics tennis issue, but **dated 1987** (designer J-L Puvilland). StampWorld gives 28 Nov 1987 ([StampWorld via search](https://stampworld.com/en/stamps/Guinea/Postage%20stamps/1980-1989?page=7)).
12. **#16 New Caledonia 18F** shows "Plesionika sp." (a deep-water shrimp), ORSTOM, 1989. It is **Yvert 576**, low value of the 1989 Marine Life set ([philatelie91](https://www.philatelie91.fr/timbres/82754-nouvelle-caledonie-n-576-oblitere-orstom-faune-marine-crevette.html); [SOTW](https://www.stampsoftheworld.co.uk/wiki/New_Caledonia_1989_Marine_Life)).
13. **#3 Poterie 30F:** no postmark is visible in Photos 1 and 2, so it may not be a used stamp at all (Photo 1).

## 3. France Liberté 0,20 and Coq 0,25: which phosphor-band, paper or printing varieties are valuable, how can the owner check, and what are they worth?

### Takeaway
- **Coq de Decaris 0,25 (1331).** It has no phosphor bands. The variety that matters is **1331d, the 1962 "coq fluo" on fluorescent paper** that glows bright yellow under UV. Yvert's used cote is **€65**; dealers sell used copies for about **€15–26**; mint copies run into the hundreds. Also look at the back for a red or green control number (coil stamps 1331b/1331c, about **€7 used**).
- **Liberté 0,20 (2181).** The normal stamp has **one phosphor bar at the right**. A copy with no bar ("sans phosphore"), a shifted bar or half bars is a variety. Used examples of such varieties are worth only about **€0.50–2**; the big values apply to mint copies.

### Cited Findings
**Phosphor bars: background**
- French definitives carry phosphor bars that are almost invisible to the eye but clear under UV light. They are used for automatic mail sorting. Varieties such as missing, shifted or broken bars can multiply value ([Delcampe Magazine, "Variétés de barres phosphorescentes"](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0240/8591/files/delcampe-magazine-3-24-30.pdf)).
- From 1977, almost all definitives carry one or two bars, printed on the white side margins and narrowed from 3 mm to 1.5 mm. Non-urgent-rate stamps have one bar; urgent-rate stamps have two, one left and one right ([same article](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0240/8591/files/delcampe-magazine-3-24-30.pdf)).
- **"Sans Pho"** stamps (no bars at all) are the most sought-after phosphor varieties. For 1970–2010, about 40% of definitive types are known without bars (51% of sheet stamps, 23% of booklet stamps) and 8% of commemoratives. The article shows a vertical pair of Libertés de Gandon "sans Pho" caused by a sideways shift of the bars ([same article](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0240/8591/files/delcampe-magazine-3-24-30.pdf)).

**Liberté 0,20**
- The 0,20 émeraude is recorded with "**1 barre à droite**" (one bar at right), price guide €0.10 used ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/825/1982-sans-legende-particuliere-type-liberte-de-gandon)).
- Comparable "sans phosphore" Liberté values in the same guide:
  - 10c without phosphor: €60 MNH, **€0.50 used** ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/7751/1982-sans-legende-particuliere-type-liberte-de-gandon)).
  - 40c without phosphor: €40 MNH, **€0.50 used** ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/3767/1982-sans-legende-particuliere-type-liberte-de-gandon)).
  - No 0,20 "sans phosphore" entry was found there.
- An eBay seller offers a 2181 with "2 demi bandes phos" (two half bars) at **€1.99 asking** ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/310914581092)).

**Coq de Decaris 0,25**
- No phosphor bars ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/3034/1962-sans-legende-particuliere-coq-de-decaris)).
- phil-ouest lists the varieties 1331a non dentelé (imperforate), 1331b red number on the back, 1331c green number on the back, and **1331d "papier spécial fluor jaune vif aux UV"** ([phil-ouest](https://www.phil-ouest.com/Timbre.php?Nom_timbre=Coq_Decaris_1331C)).
- phil-ouest says the stamp was used for trials of fluorescent paper to test envelope-facing machines, and that the trial sheets were sold in several large post offices ([phil-ouest](https://www.phil-ouest.com/Timbre.php?Nom_timbre=Coq_Decaris_1331N)).
- **The "coq fluo" story** (Delcampe Magazine):
  - In 1962 La Poste ran two trial printings on fluorescent paper using the current 25c Coq and sold them over the counter without telling anyone. Most were used on mail and thrown away.
  - Maury 2016 values the mint coq fluo at **€1,300 for type I** (pale-yellow fluorescent paper) and **€1,100 for type II** (golden-yellow under UV), against €0.20 for the normal stamp.
  - A **used type I on a 1963 letter** is valued at **€150** (Maury 2016).
  - The article adds that undetected coq fluos probably still sit in collections and dealer stocks.
  - Source: [Delcampe Magazine](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0240/8591/files/delcampe-magazine-3-24-30.pdf).
- **Prices for used 1331d:**
  - Issoire Philatélie: "cote : 65€", price **€26.00** ([Issoire Philatélie](https://www.issoire-philatelie.com/timbres-de-france-avec-varietes-bandes-de-phosphore/54570-timbre-yvert-1331d-coq-gaulois-de-decaris-fluorescent-oblitere.html)).
  - Rhône Philatélie: "papier fluorescent jaune vif aux U.V.", used, **€15.00** (out of stock) ([Rhône Philatélie](https://rhonephilatelie.fr/timbres-postes-modernes-1961-1970/20423-timbre-coq-de-descaris-n-1331d-oblitere-papier-fluorescent-annee-1962-65.html)).
  - eBay asking prices for used copies about €13.90–36.75 (see Q1 eBay check).
- **Prices for mint 1331d:**
  - Yvert's own shop offers a mint never-hinged copy at **€490**, and sellers cite a Yvert cote of €900 mint (search snapshots of [yvert.com](https://www.yvert.com/1961-1970-du-n-1281-au-n-1662/7840-n-1331d-timbre-france-poste.html) and eBay).
  - A Balsan auction lot of a mint 1331d signed by the expert Calvès carried a **€300 estimate** ([Interencheres/Balsan](https://www.interencheres.com/en-US/art-decoration/vente-chrono-de-timbre-poste-682662/lot-87494115.html)).
- **Coil Coq with a control number:** wikitimbres "roulette" entry, **€7 used, €14 MNH** ([wikitimbres](https://www.wikitimbres.fr/timbres/5920/1962-sans-legende-particuliere-coq-de-decaris)).

### Inferences
- **How to check, Liberté:**
  - Use a UV lamp in a dark room; a banknote-checker or "blacklight" torch is fine. A normal 0,20 shows **one glowing vertical bar along the right-hand white margin**.
  - No bar anywhere = possible "sans phosphore". A bar in the middle, on the left, cut in half, or two bars = phosphor variety.
  - Price evidence for used copies is low: €0.50–2 (comparable entries and an eBay asking price). Keep any such copy, but it will not be valuable.
  - Used phosphor varieties sell for little partly because a cancel and soaking make them harder to authenticate than mint sheet positions. This is my reasoning, not a sourced statement.
- **How to check, Coq:**
  1. Under UV, look at the whole paper including margins and perforation teeth. Genuine 1331d paper glows **bright yellow** (pale yellow for type I, golden for type II). Ordinary Coq paper stays dull.
  2. Compare it side by side with another 1960s French stamp.
  3. Turn the stamp over and look for a small red or green number (coil control number: 1331b/1331c).
  4. Optical brighteners from washing powder can make paper glow bluish-white. That is not the yellow fluo glow. This is general philatelic caution and not sourced. Any positive result should be confirmed by an expert before sale.
- The owner's Coq (Photo 11) looks like an ordinary copy in the photo, but **fluorescence cannot be seen in a normal photograph**, so only a UV test can rule out 1331d.

### Gaps
- No Yvert or Maury value was found specifically for a **used 0,20 Liberté without phosphor**, or for used 1331b/1331c as separate items. The wikitimbres €7 coil value is not split by red and green number.
- No current (2025/2026) Maury figure for the coq fluo was found. The €1,300/€1,100/€150 figures are from Maury 2016 as quoted by Delcampe Magazine.

## 4. Are the Senegal stamps likely cancelled-to-order (CTO) rather than postally used, and how does that affect value?

### Takeaway
Most of the owner's Senegal stamps show signs of real postal use: on piece, full dated circular postmarks such as "10 12 1988", "DAKAR" strikes, and heavy or messy cancels. CTO copies of some 1987 Senegal issues do exist in the trade. The Guinea 150F is the one most likely to be CTO. For stamps of this period the price difference is a matter of cents, so CTO status hardly changes the value. It matters mainly to collectors who want genuinely used copies.

### Cited Findings
- A CTO stamp is "a stamp the issuing postal service has cancelled (marked as used), but has not traveled through the post" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancelled-to-order)).
- Wikipedia's recognition signs: a neat cancel, usually in one corner and without a town name; intact gum; some CTOs come from whole sheets cancelled and sold as remainders ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancelled-to-order)).
- Countries that issue many topical stamps for collectors usually sell most of them as CTO. Many collectors do not regard CTOs as truly used. Stanley Gibbons prices some modern issues only as CTO because genuinely used copies are hard to find ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancelled-to-order)).
- An eBay seller offers Senegal's 1987 Christmas set (Scott 757–760, which includes the owner's Noël 1987 design) as "USED **CTO** SET OF 4" for US$1.40. CTO copies of at least this Senegal issue circulate ([eBay Senegal listings](https://www.ebay.com/b/Senegalese-Postage-Stamps/166780/bn_27087845)).
- **What the photos show:**
  - Senegal Lutte 165F pair and Argentine overprint: **on pieces of envelope** with circular datestamps across them.
  - World Food Day 145F: datestamp reading "10 12 1988" (December 1988).
  - Industries stamp: "DAK…" (Dakar) postmark.
  - Sommet de la Francophonie copies: heavy, smeared black cancels.
  - Guinea 150F: violet circular datestamp ending "…88" at its left and lower edge.
  - Poterie 30F: **no visible postmark** at all.
  - Source: Photos 1–3, 10.

### Inferences
- **Likely postally used:** on-piece stamps, datestamps falling months or years after issue (for example, the World Food Day stamp cancelled Dec 1988), and town-named Dakar strikes all point to real postal use.
- **Possibly CTO:** the Guinea 150F (a topical Olympic issue with a partial violet corner cancel) and any Senegal copy with a neat partial corner cancel.
- **Simple test** for stamps the owner can lift safely (stamps that are only hinged):
  - **Full, smooth original gum** on the back plus a neat corner cancel → CTO.
  - **No gum, or paper remnants** → postally used.
  - Stamps **glued flat to the album page** should not be forced off; peeling them causes thins, which hurt value more than CTO status.
- **Effect on value:** for 1980s Senegal and Guinea the gap between CTO and postally used is a matter of cents, so it does not change the conclusion. Postally used copies on cover, especially with clear Dakar datestamps, are slightly more attractive to postal-history collectors (inference).

### Gaps
- No source quantifying how much 1985–1989 Senegal or 1987 Guinea material reached the market as CTO.
- No catalogue note found on whether Yvert or Scott price these specific Senegal or Guinea issues differently as CTO.

#### Flagged — worth a closer look (step-by-step)
1. **Coq de Decaris 0,25 (Photo 11): fluorescent paper (Yvert 1331d)?** *Potential: used €15–30 retail, Yvert €65; not more than about €150 even on an original 1963 letter.*
   1. Get a UV lamp. A cheap 365 nm "blacklight" torch or a banknote checker is enough.
   2. In a dark room, shine it on the Coq and, beside it, on any other 1960s French stamp, for example the other French stamps in the album.
   3. **Positive result:** the whole Coq paper (margins and perforation teeth included) glows clearly **bright yellow**, pale yellow (type I) or golden (type II), while the comparison stamp stays dull.
   4. If positive, leave the stamp on the page. Do not soak it, and do not wash it in anything containing optical brighteners. Get it checked by a dealer or expert before pricing it. Genuine copies are often sold with an expert's signature, as with the Calvès-signed lot at Balsan.
2. **Coq de Decaris 0,25: coil control number on the back (1331b red / 1331c green)?** *Potential: about €7 used.* Only possible if the stamp is just hinged. Lift the hinge and look for a small printed number on the back. Do not force a stamp that is glued down.
3. **Liberté 0,20 × 2 (Photos 1–3): phosphor bar check.** *Potential: about €0.50–2 each even if a variety.*
   1. Under UV, a normal copy shows **one bar along the right margin**.
   2. Note any copy with no bar, a bar in the wrong place, a half bar or two bars. A missing bar ("sans phosphore") is the most interesting.
   3. Keep any such copy for a variety collector. It will not reach US$10 used.
4. **Senegal "Argentine 3 – R.F.A. 2" 125F (Photo 10): overprint check.** *Potential: normal copy under US$1. Error copies are undocumented.*
   1. With a magnifier, confirm the overprint is red, upright, single and complete: "ARGENTINE 3" over "R.F.A. 2", in the yellow band left of "125f".
   2. Upside-down, doubled or shifted text, or a missing overprint, would be unusual. In that case show it to a dealer before doing anything.
5. **Senegal "Poterie du Sénégal" 30F (Photos 1–2): no postmark visible.** Check the back.
   - Full gum and no cancel: it is actually **unused (mint)**.
   - No gum: "used without postmark".
   - Either way it is worth cents, but it should be described correctly.
6. **Guinea 150F and any neatly corner-cancelled Senegal stamp: CTO check.** Look at the back: full original gum points to CTO. This does not change value materially; it only matters for describing the lot honestly.

- €1 is assumed to be about US$1.1–1.2 (approximate, not sourced). On that basis US$10 is about €8.5–9. Only a confirmed coq fluo clears it at retail.
