# Italy, Netherlands, Scandinavia, Spain, Czechoslovakia, Brazil and Cyprus (1940s–1990s): identification and value of the family's used stamps

## 1. What is each stamp, what are its catalogue numbers, used catalogue value and realistic selling price?

### Takeaway
This group holds **37 stamps plus one cover**: 19 Italy, 8 Netherlands, 1 Denmark, 1 Norway, 2 Spain, 2 Czechoslovakia, 1 Brazil, and 3 Cyprus stamps on one envelope. Every stamp, as a normal copy, is a common definitive or commemorative. Where a catalogue value could be found, it is below US$1 per used copy. Realistically each stamp sells for a few cents to about US$0.50, and normally only as part of a bulk lot. The only things that could change that are confirmed varieties or postal forgeries (Section 2), the RSI stamp's condition and spelling check (Section 3), and the Cyprus cover kept intact (Section 5).

How the photos were read: I cropped each stamp from the full-resolution JPEGs, then zoomed, rotated and boosted contrast so the inscriptions and edges could be read. Photos 1–3 show one bag, so a stamp seen in more than one of them is counted once. Values are per copy unless stated. Numbers marked "(inferred)" were worked out from neighbouring catalogue numbers and not confirmed directly. "Realistic selling price" is my own estimate from the cited catalogue and market evidence, not a quoted figure.

### Corrections to the preliminary identifications
1. **Pink pair (photo 3)** is the 300 L, but it shows the **Castello Normanno-Svevo, Bari** (Sass. 1518), not an unnamed 300 L ([Landmans 1980 listing](https://www.ilpostalista.it/PDF/landmansQ80-2.pdf)).
2. **450 L (photo 5)** shows the **Castello di Bosa** (Sass. 1521).
3. **Red "20" (photo 5)** is confirmed as RSI "Monumenti distrutti". More precisely it is the **20c Bologna, Loggia dei Mercanti, first type** ("20" bottom right). The second type ("20" top right) is inscribed POSTE ITALIANE ([Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/itasoloc.htm)). **No cancel is visible**, so it may be unused rather than used.
4. **Czech "1,20 Kčs leaf"** is the **1945 Linden Leaf definitive 1.20 K** (Pofis 378, Scott 260), and it is stuck to the card upside down.
5. **Czech portrait** is the **1945 Portrait issue 50h T. G. Masaryk** (Pofis 414, Scott 301), not an unidentified person.
6. **Brazil "Campos S[ales]"** is the **1967 20-centavo Campos Salles** (Scott 1064). The value reads "20 c", not Cr$ 1,20.
7. **Norway posthorn** is a **5-kroner** posthorn from the 1992 multicoloured kroner set (NK 1156–1160), not a classic øre posthorn.
8. **Brown-violet Dutch numeral (photo 1)**: its value is hidden by a "postcode" slogan cancel. Colour and layout match the 65c. Like the **10c and 65c in photo 11, it is a coil stamp** (straight top and bottom edges: NVPH "A" numbers).
9. **Beatrix "2G"** is confirmed: 1982 Struycken design, NVPH 1245, or 1245A if it is the coil.
10. **Juliana 45c** is the 1953 "en profil" design, NVPH 628.
11. **Cyprus cover**: the postmark date **3.5.1983 is the first day of issue of Europa 1983**. The 1982 Europa 175m shows Raphael's "Conversion of Sergius Paulus". The inscription "L. SERGIVS PAVLVS … CHRISTIANAM FIDEM …" can be read on the stamp.
12. Additions: the 600 L pair (photo 10) has a black blot over "SIRMIONE" on the right stamp, probably cancel ink. I found **no other stamps of these countries** in the seven photos beyond those listed.

## 2. Italian Castelli: which varieties are valuable, and how would the owner check?

### Takeaway
The Castelli series is famous for **many varieties**: missing or shifted colours, imperforates, perforation shifts, rejoined paper, and fluorescent versus non-fluorescent paper. Several values were also **counterfeited to defraud the post office**. In the owner's holding, the useful checks are a UV lamp on the **100 L** (scarce non-fluorescent printing) and the **300 L pair** (fluorescent-paper variety sold with certificates), and a perforation gauge and loupe on the **1000 L** (postal forgeries known). On every value, compare multiples for missing colours or shifted perforations. A normal copy is worth cents; only a confirmed variety would exceed US$10.

### Cited Findings
- A specialist series of articles on Castelli varieties (Ketty Borgogno, *Il Foglio* no. 190, Filatelia Subalpina) reports that the **"salto del pettine"** (comb-perforator jump) occurs on all Castelli values. It produces oversized stamps with an extra-large tooth ("dentone") — [Il Postalista / Il Foglio 190](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/027.pdf)
- **380 L**: known with colour differences, faint printing, imperforate, black missing or partly missing, and slight print shifts — [Il Foglio 190](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/027.pdf)
- **450 L**: copies exist with only the slate colour printed, with only two colours (slate and red, green or blue), without red, misregistered, with shifted perforations, imperforate on one side, and on rejoined paper ("carta ricongiunta"). The 450 L was **reprinted in rotogravure on 21 February 1994**, and that second issue also has missing colours and imperforates — [Il Foglio 190](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/027.pdf)
- **350 L** was the first value counterfeited: discovered in Monza, April 1993, perf 14 × 13¼, offset on non-fluorescent, slightly yellowish paper; the "falso di Milano" is shown used on piece — [Il Foglio 190](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/027.pdf)
- **600 L**: two colour shades (azure; turquoise tending to green). Very heavy green is uncommon, almost-absent green exists, and the total absence of green ("**castello grigio**") is noteworthy. Copies on rejoined paper, imperforate or partly perforated are "not easy to find". Coloured-background copies come from a dirty cylinder, not coloured paper. A "partial print" can be faked by lifting ink with tape — [Il Postalista, part VII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/033.pdf)
- **650 L**: shades still under study. Misregistration makes the "650" look a different height; missing yellow, shifted yellow and missing black are recorded; there is a constant green flaw called "**paracadute**" (parachute) — [part VII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/033.pdf)
- **Paper**: the 700 L was also printed on **non-fluorescent paper with stelle II watermark, "like the 100 lire"**. Unlike the 750 L, the non-fluorescent 700 L **and 100 L are "much more difficult to find"**. The 500 L (Milan, 1992) and 700 L (Naples) were forged to defraud the post office — [part VII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/033.pdf)
- **1000 L**: shades from heavier or lighter blue. Copies exist without the "1", without the last zero, with faint central zeros, or without the first or second "I" of ITALIA. Also known: faint castle, strongly shifted perforations, misregistered colours, and the recurring "**merli rotti**" (broken battlements). The **"castello blu"** is called the best variety, but used copies are often **artificially faded fakes**: the meadow turns turquoise, and the screen dots of the "missing" colour still show under a loupe — [Il Postalista, part VIII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/037.pdf)
- **1000 L postal forgeries**: the "falso di Milano" has coarse printing, **perf 13**, and stars drawn on the back (or none). The "falso di Verzuolo" is **perf about 12** with no watermark, printed like the forged 500 L — [part VIII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/037.pdf)
- Genuine 600 L: comb perf 14¼ × 13¼, stars IV watermark, offset plus recess — [allnumis](https://allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/italy/castle-fortress/600-lire-1980-castello-sacligero-sirmione-12084)
- A **1980 Castelli 300 L "on fluorescent paper", with a certificate**, is offered by a dealer — [eBay.de listing title](https://www.ebay.de/itm/406050593204). A certified Castelli variety (30 L "n.1569Db", Ferrario certificate) is also offered — [search summary of eBay listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/404187433657)
- **Coil Castelli** ("Castelli bobine", 22 Sept 1980) were for vending machines. They came as **30 L + 170 L and 30 L + 120 L** pairs, **imperforate horizontally**, and in a smaller size — [Landmans](https://www.ilpostalista.it/PDF/landmansQ80-2.pdf). Used coil pairs with a number on the back are offered — [eBay.it](https://www.ebay.it/itm/333823990907)

### Inferences
- **No coil Castelli are present.** All Italian stamps in the photos are full-size sheet stamps perforated on all four sides; the pairs show perforations between the stamps.
- The owner's best chances, in order:
  1. 100 L Ischia under UV (scarce non-fluorescent printing).
  2. 300 L Bari pair under UV (fluorescent variety).
  3. 1000 L Montagnana: perforation gauge for a postal forgery, and a loupe for missing digits or letters.
  4. 600 L ×3 and 650 L ×2: missing green, yellow or black.
- The black blot over "SIRMIONE" on one 600 L is most likely cancel ink or grease from the postmark; a missing word would be a printing variety. A 10× loupe will show whether printed letters lie under the black.
- Even a confirmed variety needs an expert certificate (Italian periti such as those named on the listings: Ferrario, Raybaudi, Diena; the article recommends Nicola Luciano Cipriani, peritocipriani.it) before it sells for real money.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain current Unificato or Sassone catalogue values for any Castelli variety, including the non-fluorescent 100 L, the fluorescent 300 L and the 1000 L postal forgeries. The only prices found are eBay **asking** prices (Section 7).
- The articles do not say which printings of the 300 L are fluorescent versus non-fluorescent. A UV result therefore has to be compared against reference copies or an expert.
- Parts I–V of the Il Postalista series, which cover the 50, 100, 120, 200 and 300 L in detail, were not located.

## 3. The red "20" (RSI 1944 "Monumenti distrutti"): printings, perforations, varieties and values

### Takeaway
The stamp is the RSI **20c carmine "Loggia dei Mercanti, Bologna"** of the 1944 Destroyed Monuments set, **first type** ("20" bottom right). The set is very common. This copy shows **no visible cancellation** and is glued to cardboard, so as it stands it is worth well under US$1. The one thing worth a minute's check is the **"SOCIAIE" spelling error** (Sassone 504aa), which is known on the 20c. In the photo the word appears to read SOCIALE correctly, so it is probably not the error.

### Cited Findings
- The 20c Monumenti distrutti shows the "Loggia dei mercanti" in Bologna — [Klaseboer, Italian Social Republic](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/italysoc.htm); [Waymarking](https://www.waymarking.com/waymarks/WM18Q0F_Palazzo_della_Mercanzia_Bologna_Italy)
- There are two types: first type "20" bottom right, second type "20" upper right. The "Lire 1,20" on 20c and "2 LIRE" on 25c surcharges were applied to the second type, inscribed "POSTE ITALIANE" — [Klaseboer local overprints](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/itasoloc.htm); [Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/italysoc.htm)
- "These stamps are very common"; the 50c violet has been forged — [Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/italysoc.htm)
- The 20c was issued 22 August 1944 and printed by the Officina Carte Valori, Novara. Rome and Novara printings are marked "X" and "Y"; the Novara print is without watermark; Michel 650Y–661Y for the set and Scott 22–31 — [Stamps of the World](https://www.stampsoftheworld.co.uk/wiki/Italy_1944_Destroyed_momuments_-_II) (via search snippet; the page itself refused direct fetch)
- Further Monumenti distrutti values on paper without watermark (Jan–Mar 1945) are Sassone 505 (25c emerald green), 510 (1.25 L) and 511 (3 L) — [Landmans RSI catalogue](https://www.ilpostalista.it/PDF/landmansrsi.pdf)
- The error "**SOCIAIE** instead of SOCIALE" is catalogued as **Sassone 504aa** — [italianstamps.co.uk RSI page](https://italianstamps.co.uk/rsi). A dealer lists "Monumenti Distrutti 2° Em. **20 c** Varietà SOCIAIE" — [eBay.de listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/254836184297)
- A used Monumenti distrutti stamp is offered at €1 plus €2 shipping (asking) — [search summary of eBay.it listings](https://www.ebay.it/itm/273835753395)

### Inferences
- Sassone 505/510/511 are the 25c, 1.25 L and 3 L, and the SOCIAIE error on the 20c is 504aa. So the 20c is most likely **Sassone 504**, with the series running roughly 502–511.
- The stamp's surface shows no postmark ink. With a clear postmark it would count as postally used. Genuinely used RSI stamps with legible 1944–45 dates are generally preferred by collectors, but I found no figure to quantify that.
- Printing or perforation sub-varieties (Rome vs Novara paper) cannot be checked while the stamp is glued to cardboard. For a stamp of this value it is not worth soaking it off.

### Gaps
- No current Sassone value for the 20c (mint or used) or for the SOCIAIE error was found.
- Whether the 20c exists in both Rome (watermarked) and Novara (unwatermarked) printings is unclear. The Michel list in the snippet shows "655" without an X/Y split, which hints that only one paper type is listed, but I could not confirm this.

## 4. Dutch Crouwel numerals, Juliana and Beatrix: phosphor, paper or coil varieties worth more than about €5?

### Takeaway
No phosphor or paper variety worth more than €5 could be identified for these values. The Crouwel 10c and 65c, and the hidden-value numeral in photo 1, are **coil stamps** (NVPH 1109A and 1116A, straight top and bottom edges). That is a separate catalogue listing but still a cheap stamp. Only coil stamps carrying a **printed number on the back** (rugnummer) are sought after. Even those were not shown in any source to exceed €5 used.

### Cited Findings
- Crouwel 10c coil NVPH 1109A is "imperforated top and bottom" on phosphorescent paper — [allnumis](https://allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/netherlands/numerals-figures/10-centi-numeral-type-crouwel-coil-51097). The coil numbers run NVPH 1108A–1118A — [Corinphila](https://corinphila.nl/en/node/681090)
- Dealers sell "Crouwel 1108a–1118a van de rol alle zegels met nummer" (coil stamps all with number) and a "rolzegel 6-strip 1116Rc" — [eBay.nl search page](https://www.ebay.nl/sch/i.html?_nkw=crouwel+zegels+1108+1118+postfris) (via search snippet)
- A certified Crouwel 45c "with variety" has been sold at Catawiki, so certified varieties exist in the series — [Catawiki lot](https://www.catawiki.com/en/l/99865496-netherlands-1976-number-series-crouwel-45-cents-with-variety-and-certificate)
- The 65c (NVPH 1116) and the Beatrix 2G (NVPH 1245/1245A) are on phosphorescent paper — [allnumis 65c](https://www.allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/netherlands/numerals-figures/65-cents-1986-figure-7910); [allnumis 2G](https://www.allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/netherlands/queen-beatrix/2-gulden-1982-21678)

A used 1953 45c sold on eBay for US$0.99 — [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/266029014331) (via search snippet)

### Inferences
- How to tell coil from sheet:
  - Crouwel numerals: coil = straight (unperforated) **top and bottom** edges, perforations at left and right.
  - Beatrix 2G coil: perforations at **top and bottom only**, straight sides.
  - In photo 11 the 2G's side edges look straight, which suggests NVPH 1245A. Booklet stamps can also show straight edges, so check against the catalogue.
- The back number on coil stamps is printed on the reverse at intervals. It can only be seen if the stamp is removed from the album page, so check only loose copies or ones that lift off easily.

### Gaps
- No NVPH used values for 1109A, 1116A, 1245/1245A or 628 were obtained, and none for back-numbered coils.
- I found no source listing a non-phosphor or other paper variety for the 65c, 10c, 2G or Juliana 45c.

## 5. The Cyprus cover: realistic value as a cover, and is it worth more intact?

### Takeaway
**Keep it intact.** The three stamps are common and would be worth well under US$1 each off paper. As a cover it is more interesting:
- It is cancelled **3 May 1983, the first day of issue of the Europa 1983 stamps** (a genuine commercial first-day usage, not a made-up first-day cover).
- It combines two Europa issues with the **compulsory Refugee Fund tax stamp**.
- It is franked in **mils a few months before Cyprus moved to cents** in 1983.
- It was addressed in Arabic.

My estimate is about **US$5–15 retail**, and less if sold to a dealer. I found no sold comparables.

### Cited Findings
- Cyprus Europa 1983 stamps were issued **3.5.1983**; the 50m shows a Cypro-syllabic inscription — [Let's Talk Stamps](https://letstalkstamps.blogspot.com/2014/03/europa-1983-cyprus-great-achievements.html). The 50m depicts a 6th-century-BC tombstone from Yialia (Paphos) naming Timokypra, read right to left — [Let's Talk Stamps](https://letstalkstamps.blogspot.com/2011/12/relics-of-past.html)
- Stampworld lists the 1983 Europa 50M (its no. 593) with perforation 14¾ — [Stampworld Cyprus 1983](https://stampworld.com/en/stamps/Cyprus-(Greek)/Postage%20stamps/?year=1983) (via search snippet)
- Cyprus Europa 1982 was issued 3 May 1982 (40m companion: SG 586) — [Colnect](https://colnect.com/en/stamps/stamp/67488-Byzantine_battleship_Portrait_of_Emperor-Europa_CEPT_1982_-_Historical_Events-Cyprus). Cyprus also uses "The Conversion of the Proconsul Sergius Paulus" as a historical-scene image — [Colnect phonecard](https://colnect.com/en/phonecards/phonecard/30665-The_Conversion_of_the_Proconsul_Sergius_Paulus-Historical_Scenes-Cyta-Cyprus)
- Since 10 January 1977 all Refugee Fund stamps share the same design (a figure squatting before barbed wire). The stamp is stuck on all letters and parcels, with proceeds going to refugees of 1974 — [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2023/03/26/cyprus-refugee-stamp-the-story-of-a-symbol)
- 1983 mils stamps were surcharged in cents (e.g. "5c on 50M"), showing the change of currency in 1983 — [TouchStamps](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/22997/black-surcharge-5c-on-50m)

### Inferences
- Postage is 175m + 50m = 225 mils, plus the 10m compulsory tax.
- Collectors of Cyprus postal history, of Europa first-day usages and of mail to the Middle East may each find the cover attractive. Soaking the stamps off would destroy that and leave about US$1–2 of common used stamps.
- The red printed Arabic word at the top of the envelope suggests a printed organisational envelope, probably from one of the organisations that mailed from Cyprus to the Arab world in the 1980s. This is speculative; a sender's imprint on the back would settle it.

### Gaps
- No catalogue or sold-price data for comparable commercial Cyprus covers of 1983 was found, so the US$5–15 range is my estimate only.
- The office name in the postmark (only "CYPRUS" is legible) and the back of the envelope were not visible.

## 6. Overall: does anything in this group plausibly exceed US$10 per stamp?

### Takeaway
**Not as normal copies.** Every catalogue figure found is under US$1 used. The only candidates for more than US$10 are unconfirmed possibilities that need checking:
1. A Castelli variety or postal forgery: non-fluorescent 100 L, fluorescent 300 L, a 1000 L forgery, missing colours or imperforates.
2. An RSI 20c with the SOCIAIE error.
3. The Cyprus cover as a whole, borderline at about US$5–15.

### Flagged — worth a closer look (step-by-step checks)
**A. Italian Castelli (all 19 Italian stamps; priority: 100 L, 300 L pair, 1000 L, 600 L ×3, 650 L pair)**
1. Work in daylight with a 10× loupe. Put stamps of the same value side by side (they have 2–3 copies of several values) and compare with a normal image online.
2. **Colours**:
   - 600 L: is the castle grey instead of green-blue (green missing)?
   - 650 L: any missing yellow or black, or a green "parachute" speck in the background?
   - 1000 L: are both "I"s of ITALIA, the "1" and all three zeros present? Are the battlements broken? Is the castle blue instead of brown?
   - 450 L: is only the slate (dark grey) printed?
   - Any value: is the black outline or value missing?
3. **Perforations**: check whether the perforation cuts into the picture, whether one tooth is oversized, and whether any edge, especially **between the two stamps of each pair**, is straight with no holes. Pairs (50 L, 300 L, 600 L, 650 L, 380/120 L) must not be separated.
4. **UV (paper)**: in a dark room, shine a **long-wave UV torch (365 nm)** on all the Castelli at once. Most should react the same way. Set aside any 100 L or 300 L that looks clearly different (dull vs. bright) from the others. Do not look into the lamp.
5. **Forgery check, 1000 L**: lay a printed perforation gauge on the edge. A genuine Castelli measures about 14 × 13¼ (600 L: 14¼ × 13¼). A **1000 L measuring 13 or about 12, with coarse dotted printing**, may be a postal forgery. A forgery is worth more than a genuine copy but needs a certificate.
6. Anything found: leave it on its paper, photograph front and back, and ask an Italian expert (perito) before offering it for sale.

**B. RSI 20c (photo 5)**
1. With a loupe, read the bottom panel. If "SOCIALE" reads "**SOCIAIE**" (the L looks like an I), it is the catalogued error Sass. 504aa.
2. Look for any postmark ink. If none, treat it as unused and of minimal value.
3. Do not soak it off the cardboard; the gain would not justify the risk.

**C. Dutch coils (photo 1 numeral; photo 11 10c, 65c and 2G)**
1. Confirm the straight edges: top and bottom for the Crouwel numerals, left and right for the 2G coil.
2. Only if a stamp lifts easily, look at the back for a small printed number (rugnummer). Back-numbered coils are the only ones collectors seek.
3. For the photo-1 numeral, tilt it under light to read the value under the slogan cancel.

**D. Cyprus cover**: keep it whole, store it flat in a paper or archival sleeve, and photograph the back for any sender's imprint or arrival postmark.

**E. Siracusana 50 L**: check under UV and, if loose, look at the stars watermark. Later printings on fluorescent paper exist in the series. This is a low-probability check.

### Cited Findings
- Catalogue levels found: Castelli 600 L and 1000 L US$0.57 used ([Stampworld](https://www.stampworld.com/en/stamps/Italy/Postage%20stamps/1980-1989)); Denmark 4.75 kr ≈ US$0.85 used ([Stampworld](https://www.stampworld.com/stamps/Denmark/Postage-stamps/g1003)); Espamer '82 ≈ US$0.26–0.29 used ([Stampworld](https://stampworld.com/en/stamps/Spain/Postage%20stamps/?year=1982))
- RSI Monumenti distrutti are "very common" — [Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol2/html/italysoc.htm)
- Varieties and postal forgeries of the Castelli are documented by specialists — [part VII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/033.pdf); [part VIII](https://www.ilpostalista.it/varieta/pdf/037.pdf)
- The Siracusana stars-watermark values of 1955 must not be confused with the same values on wheel-watermark paper (1953) or on fluorescent paper with a smaller design (1968) — [Landmans 1955 listing](https://www.ilpostalista.it/PDF/landmans_rp03.pdf). Watermark questions on Siracusana values (e.g. a 1968 100 L with second-type stars) are discussed in an Il Postalista Q&A — [Il Postalista](https://www.ilpostalista.it/coll-2/congiu01/00.pdf)

### Inferences
- Realistic total for this group without any confirmed variety: about **US$5–15 for the 37 loose stamps as a lot, plus about US$5–15 for the Cyprus cover**.
- The checks in A–E take under an hour with a loupe, a UV torch (inexpensive) and a printed perforation gauge. Done before the lot is sold, they guard against giving away a variety.

### Gaps
- Varieties cannot be confirmed from photographs. Colour shifts, UV reaction and perforation measurements need the stamps in hand.
