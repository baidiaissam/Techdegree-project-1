# Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian, Libyan and Saudi stamps in the family lot (photos 2, 3, 5, 10, 11): identification, catalogue numbers, values, varieties, eBay check

Scope: 24 copies of 21 different stamps (Morocco 16 copies, Algeria 1, Tunisia 1, Libya 2, Saudi Arabia 4). All were viewed at full resolution by cropping the photos. Research date: 15 November 2026.

Source notes, so the report writer can weigh them:
- **TouchStamps** (touchstamps.com) supplied the Michel (Mi), Scott (Sn), Yvert (Yt) and SG numbers, plus dates, perforations and printings. The site is a free community catalogue, and its Scott and SG numbers are sparse.
- **StampWorld** catalogue values (in USD) could not be fetched directly: the site returned HTTP 403 behind a Cloudflare challenge. They are quoted from search-engine summaries of StampWorld pages and are marked "via search summary".
- **Delcampe sold results** (filter `display_state=sold_items`) are the main evidence of real prices. They are actual completed sales, in € with an approximate US$ conversion shown by Delcampe.
- **Yvert values ("cote")** come only from sellers quoting them in their listing titles. I had no access to the Yvert catalogue itself.

---

## 1. What exactly is each stamp (catalogue numbers), what is its used catalogue value, and what does it realistically sell for?

### Takeaway
Every stamp in this group is a common postally used 1968–2001 issue. Catalogue values for used copies are about €0.15–0.75 (Yvert, as quoted by sellers) or US$0.26–0.56 (StampWorld). Used singles actually sell on Delcampe for about US$0.03–0.45, and several copies here are damaged or glued to cardboard. The preliminary inventory needed 17 corrections, listed below. The most important: the "1967" Saudi blue pair is really a **1992** King Abdulaziz University crest definitive, and the photo-5 "0,30 oranges" postage due is really **peaches**.

Supporting details:
- **Postage-due designer and reprints.** The Fruits postage dues were designed by Rachid Rabih. A full sheet of the 0,20 grapes is inscribed "July 1978" in the margin, so the 1974 values were reprinted without any catalogue distinction for single stamps ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Issue/Details/387965/fruits-1974)).
- **The two Hassan II types, compared on TouchStamps images.** The owner's 0,05 matches the 1968 type (Yt 536: King in red fez and white djellaba, "POSTES" written vertically under the value). It does not match the 1973 photogravure type (Yt 657, Mi 723, Scott 277, SG 344), which shows the King in a dark suit next to the royal arms ([TS 1968](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/339395/king-hassan-ii-1929-1999); [TS 1973](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/134933/king-hassan-ii)).
- **The coin stamps, compared on TouchStamps images.**
  - The owner's green 0,05 (silver coin, imprint "A. Jaouhari") matches Yt 769 of 9 Sep 1976, not Yt 756 of 26 Apr 1976 (a gold coin on mauve).
  - The owner's orange-brown 0,15 (silver coin with a zig-zag border, imprint "P. Rieunier") matches Yt 757 of 26 Apr 1976, not Yt 770 of 9 Sep 1976 (a gold coin on red).

#### Corrections to the preliminary inventory
1. **Photo 5, left edge:** the postage due is **0,30 Pêches (peaches)**, Yt Taxe 63, not oranges ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/87167/peaches)).

#### Flagged — worth a closer look (step-by-step)

**A. Saudi Holy Kaaba 10 H and 20 H (photo 11, right side). This is the only item with a plausible US$10 catalogue variety.**
1. **Measure the printed picture** (the coloured area, not the perforations) with a millimetre ruler.
   - Sheet stamps are about **26 mm wide × 21 mm high**.
   - The scarce 1986 vending-machine booklet stamps are **29 mm × 19 mm**: visibly longer and lower ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/346444/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca); [TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/1596948/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca)).
   - From the photo, the owner's two stamps have a picture ratio of about 1.2–1.3 : 1, which matches the sheet stamps (26 : 21 ≈ 1.24). The booklet stamps would be about 1.5 : 1. My measurement on the photo, which is held at an angle, therefore suggests both are **ordinary sheet stamps**.
2. **Look at all four edges for a straight (unperforated) edge.** A straight edge is typical of booklet panes; this is general philatelic knowledge, not confirmed for this booklet.
3. **Count perforation teeth** with a perforation gauge, or count the holes along 2 cm.
   - About 14 holes per 2 cm = 1982 printing (comb 14 × 13¾ or line 13¾).
   - About 12 holes per 2 cm = a 1984–87 perf-12 printing (Mi D, Scott 874b for the 20 H) ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/1236766/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca)).
4. **Check for a watermark** (Michel X vs Y). Lay the stamp face down on a black surface; a drop of watermark fluid helps.
5. **Check the 10 H for a cancellation.** In the photo it shows no clear cancel, so it may be unused. That makes little difference to the value of a sheet stamp.
6. **If it is a booklet stamp** (29 × 19 mm and perf 12), it is catalogued at about US$9–11 ([SW](https://www.stampworld.com/en/stamps/Saudi-Arabia/Postage%20stamps/1980-1989?year=1986)). No completed sale of a single used booklet stamp was found, so a realistic price would be a fraction of that. If it is a sheet stamp, it is worth pennies.

**B. Morocco "Msouffa" 1,00 (photo 11)**
1. **Count the perforation holes** along the top edge over 2 cm.
   - 15 = sheet stamp, Yt 554 / Mi 598A.
   - 13 (sides 12¾) = booklet stamp, Yt 554a / Mi 598C ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/339391/msouffa); [TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/339392/msouffa)).
2. **Look for a straight edge**, which points to a booklet stamp (inference).
3. **Value if it is a booklet stamp: unknown.** No 554a sales were found, and the used sheet stamp sells for about US$0.28 ([DC](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/stamps/morocco-1956/maroc-n-554-o-costume-2193903852.html)).

**C. Algeria 0,50 "8 Mai 1945" (photo 11)**
1. **Check whether any side is unperforated** (straight). Booklet stamps are Mi 682D/E, and the "l" and "ru" suffixes point to left or right-and-bottom straight edges ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Issue/Details/263287/commemoration-du-8-mai-1945)).
2. In the photo the owner's copy looks **perforated on all four sides**, so it is the sheet stamp.
3. Even a used booklet single sold for only US$0.24 ([DC](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/stamps/algeria-1962/algerie-y-t-n-644b-oblitere-1664135767.html)). **Not worth pursuing.**

**D. Everything else.**
- The Moroccan postage dues, coin stamps, Hassan II 0,05, Mausolée, energy, horse, UNICEF and fish stamps, and the Libyan and Tunisian stamps, have no listed varieties in their sources that apply to perforated used copies.
- The owner should only check that perforations are complete, there are no thins (hold the stamp to the light: thin spots look brighter), and there are no tears or creases.
- **Do not soak the photo-5 stamps off the cardboard just to sell them.** The value gained is cents and the risk is damage (inference).

### Inferences
- The only realistic way any stamp in this group exceeds a few cents is if a Kaaba stamp turned out to be a 1986 booklet stamp. The photo geometry argues against it.
- Complete mint 1986 booklets clearly command high prices: US$99.50–400 sold, US$210–220 asked. Used singles are a different market, and no evidence of their sale prices was found.

### Gaps
- No market data was found for the Msouffa booklet stamp (Yt 554a), the Kaaba perf-12 printings (Mi 718DY, 720DX/DY, Scott 874b) or single used 1986 booklet stamps.
- The meaning of Michel's "X/Y" paper codes for the Kaaba issues is inferred from SG 1283d's note "no watermark". I could not see Michel's own definition.
- I could not confirm that the 1986 booklet stamps have straight edges.

## 5. Overall: does anything in this group plausibly exceed US$10 per stamp?

### Takeaway
**No.** As photographed, every stamp in this group is worth well under US$1 used, and most are worth a few cents. The only theoretical exception is if either Saudi Holy Kaaba stamp (10 H or 20 H) were a 1986 vending-machine booklet stamp. Those are catalogued at about US$9–11 each, and complete mint booklets have sold for US$99.50–400. However, the photo proportions indicate the owner's copies are ordinary sheet stamps, and no sale of a used single booklet stamp was found.

### Cited Findings
- Used Delcampe completed sales for the identified stamps range from €0.03 (Hassan II Yt 536, 5 Apr 2025) to about US$0.45 (coin Yt 749) ([DC](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/stamps/morocco-1956/maroc-n-536-oblitere-cote-0-20eur-roi-hassan-ii-955258031.html); [DC search](https://www.delcampe.net/en_GB/collectables/search?search_mode=all&term=maroc+749+monnaie&size=60&display_state=sold_items)).
- Yvert values quoted by sellers for these used stamps run about €0.15–0.75 (Q1 table).
- StampWorld catalogues the 1986 Saudi Holy Kaaba perf-12 booklet issue at about US$9.29 (10 H) and US$10.52–11.18 (20 H), via search summary ([SW](https://www.stampworld.com/en/stamps/Saudi-Arabia/Postage%20stamps/1980-1989?year=1986)). The 1982 sheet 10 H is US$0.27 ([SW](https://www.stampworld.com/en_US/stamps/Saudi-Arabia/Postage%20stamps/1980-1989?year=1982)).
- Sheet stamps measure 26 × 21 mm and booklet stamps 29 × 19 mm ([TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/346444/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca); [TS](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/1596948/holy-ka-aba-in-mecca)).

### Inferences
- **Realistic total value for this group: roughly US$3–6** if each stamp were sold individually at Delcampe prices, and much less as part of a bulk lot. The figure is inferred from the sold prices above. It ignores fees and postage, and ignores condition, which pulls several copies down.
- **The only action that could change the picture** is the measurement and perforation check of the two Kaaba stamps (Q3, flag A). Even then, a used booklet single would realistically fetch a few dollars rather than the full catalogue figure. That is an inference: no used-single sales were found.

### Gaps
- No direct sale record exists for a used single 1986 Kaaba booklet stamp.
- eBay sold data could not be obtained (Q4).
- Yvert used values were not available for several stamps (Q1 Gaps).
