# Allied-occupation Germany 1945–1948 (AM Post, Control Council numerals and pictorials) and one Bohemia & Moravia stamp: identification, value and varieties

Researcher notes, 15 Nov 2026. Scope: the AM Post, 1946 Control Council numeral and 1947–48 Control Council pictorial stamps, plus the Bohemia & Moravia stamp, in photos 2, 3, 6 and 7. Bundespost (1949+) stamps on the same pages are excluded because another researcher covers them.

How I worked: I viewed all four photos at full resolution, cropping and zooming each stamp in my group. Photos 6 and 7 carry an EXIF rotation, and my crops correct for it. For prices I used catalogue and handbook pages and a large German dealer's live price list (phila-versand.de, retail prices for single stamps, fetched 15 Nov 2026). For current eBay listings I used a PicClick mirror of eBay.de, captured the same day; eBay itself blocked automated access. I could not reach a current Michel catalogue. "Michel" figures below are either quoted by sellers in their listing titles (treat them as unverified) or are absent. **Currency:** EUR unless stated. €10 is roughly US$11–12 at an assumed rate of €1 ≈ US$1.15, which I did not verify for Nov 2026.

## Q1. What exactly is each stamp (Michel, Scott), what is its used catalogue value, and what does it realistically sell for?

### Takeaway
The group contains **16 stamps**:
- 3 AM Post (3, 4 and 10 Pf)
- 6 Control Council numerals of 1946 (6, 12, 20, 40, 50 and 75 Pf)
- 6 Control Council "worker" pictorials of 1947–48 (2, 8, 15, 16, 24 and 50 Pf)
- 1 Bohemia & Moravia 2.50 K St. Vitus Cathedral stamp of 1944

In their ordinary form, every one of them is worth only cents: dealers retail the common used versions at about €0.45–0.90 each, and they sell for less, usually in bulk. Real money depends on a stamp turning out to be a specific scarce perforation, paper or shade variety, which only a perforation gauge, magnification and usually an expert certificate can confirm. No overprints are visible on any of the 1946–48 stamps.

### Inferences
- The realistic selling price of each common used stamp here is about €0.05–0.30, mostly as part of a lot. A whole used set of 20–27 stamps retails for only €27.50–42.50, and much of that is the high values (sources above).
- A dealer buying stock would pay a fraction of retail. That is general trade practice and is not documented in my sources.
- The Scott numbers marked "inferred" assume Scott runs in the same order as Michel. That fits the 530–556 range, but I did not check it in Scott.
- The 12 Pf red is most likely Mi 920, based on three clues:
  1. 920 is the April 1946 issue and is the 12 Pf in the 1947 booklet panes ("920+923+920" — [touchstamps](https://touchstamps.com/Stamp/Details/1215068/1st-allied-control-council-issue)).
  2. Its listed shade varieties include a "pink tint to numeral" ([briefhistory](https://briefhistory.co.uk/1946/mi.911-937-joint-issue)).
  3. It is found on many 1946–47 covers.

  No source I could open names the colours of 919 and 920 outright, so the owner should confirm this in a Michel catalogue.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain current **Michel catalogue values** for the common used versions; Michel is paywalled and dealer pages don't show it. The only Michel figures are numbers quoted by sellers.
- I could not verify Scott numbers for the 1947–48 pictorials or for the colour assignment of the two 12 Pf numerals.
- The colour name of the Bohemia & Moravia 2.50 K was not confirmed; it photographs as grey to slate-violet.

## Q2. AM Post 3, 4 and 10 Pf: how do the Washington, London and Brunswick printings differ, which combinations are scarce, how can an owner check, and what are they worth?

### Takeaway
1. **Perforation fineness first.** About 14 holes per 2 cm means London. About 11 or 11½ means Washington or Brunswick.
2. **Then the design.** Brunswick has a **white triangle** in the ornament to the left of the "P" of PFENNIG; Washington lacks it.
3. **Then the paper.** Washington paper is thick; Brunswick paper is thin.

Valuable combinations for these three values:
- **10 Pf Brunswick, perf 11½ all round (Mi 22 D)**: a genuine rarity, offered at about €1,200 used with a certificate.
- **3 Pf Brunswick in the blue-violet shade with perforation C or D (17 b C/D)**: €35–290.
- **3 Pf London, perforation A on y paper or perforation G (10 A y, 10 G)**: €23–35.
- **4 Pf Brunswick, perforation B (18 B)**: up to about €21.

Everything else is €0.45–5. Visually, the owner's 3 Pf is not blue-violet and is heavily toned. The 4 Pf and 10 Pf look coarsely perforated (gauge about 11), so London is unlikely for them. That is a low-confidence reading of the photos.

### Cited Findings
**How the printings differ**
- **Printing method.**
  - Washington and Brunswick are offset; London is photogravure (rotogravure), and its screen dots are visible under magnification. — [ArGe AM-Post FAQ](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)
  - Conflicting source: a dealer page says the Washington printing is rotary gravure and looks "very clean". — [Briefmarkenmesse Essen](https://www.briefmarkenmesse-essen.de/?p=550). I would trust the specialist society (ArGe) over the dealer.
- **Design detail.**
  - Brunswick has a white triangle in the ornament left of the "P" of "PFENNIG"; Washington has no white patch there. — [Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol1/html/bizone.htm); [ArGe FAQ](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)
  - worldstampsproject puts it as Washington having a "colored triangle" and Brunswick a "white triangle" left of the right-hand P of PFENNIG. — [worldstampsproject](https://worldstampsproject.org/germany-allied-occupation-am-post/)
  - The Brunswick printing shows "more detail in the scrollwork". — [Ken's blog](https://kensstampcollection.blogspot.com/2023/09/distinguishing-german-am-post-printings.html)
- **Size.** The Washington design (Mi 1–9) is "4 mm breiter" (4 mm wider) than the small-format Brunswick printing. Comparing with a reference stamp settles doubtful cases. — [VPS 1894, Part 1](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-1-die-basissammlung.html)

**Perforations**
- **Washington:** line perforation 11:11 only, with no gauge varieties ([VPS 1894, Part 4](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html)). worldstampsproject gives "wide, line 10¾–11" ([worldstampsproject](https://worldstampsproject.org/germany-allied-occupation-am-post/)), a minor conflict.
- **London:** line perforations of 14¼, 14½ and 14¾ in seven catalogued combinations. Letter G = 14¾:14½. For Mi 15 b, copies gauging 14¾:14¾ exist, but the BPP specialist does not accept an "H" and marks them "G". — [VPS 1894, Part 4](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html)
  - "All London stamps have narrow perforation." — [ArGe FAQ](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)
  - "Enge Zähnung ist immer englischer Druck" (narrow perforation always means the London printing). — [VPS Part 1](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-1-die-basissammlung.html)
- **Brunswick:** line perforations of 11 or 11½ holes per 2 cm, in four catalogued gauges: L 11:11, L 11:11½, L 11½:11 and L 11½:11½. — [VPS Part 4](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html)
  - Letter codes: A = 11:11 and B = 11:11½. Source: search-result summary of ArGe AM-Post newsletter reading samples ([Leseprobe 115](https://arge-am-post.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Leseprobe-115.pdf)); I did not open the PDF myself.
  - A seller titles a stamp "17b B, gezähnt 11:11,5". — [eBay 234270425157](https://www.ebay.de/itm/234270425157)
  - D = 11½ all round: "Michel 22D (10 Pf, L 11½)" — [VPS Part 4](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-4-zaehnungen.html); and "blauviolett 11 1/2 … 17 b Dz" — [eBay 167727130653](https://www.ebay.de/itm/167727130653).

**Papers**
- **Washington:**
  - x paper: always white and very smooth.
  - z paper: rougher, often cloudy.
  - y paper: much thinner, like the Brunswick paper.
  - The x and z papers are thicker than the Brunswick paper.
  - Only the 6 Pf (Mi 13) has x paper in the London printing.
  - Source: [ArGe FAQ](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)
- **Paper thickness by print run** ([worldstampsproject](https://worldstampsproject.org/germany-allied-occupation-am-post/)):
  - Washington 2nd run (20 Jun 1945): 3, 4 and 10 Pf on thick smooth white paper (0.11 mm), plus all values on thick rough greyish-brownish paper with wood splinters (0.10–0.12 mm, dull colours).
  - London: thin paper, 0.065–0.10 mm.
  - Brunswick "Alfelder" paper: 0.07–0.095 mm.
- Forum experts say paper cannot be reliably identified from a scan, and the American x paper is much smoother than z. — [philaforum](https://philaforum.com/forum/thread/9495-am-post/)

**Expertising and fakes**
- Andreas Schlegel and Andreas Wehner (BPP) examine everything AM Post. — [ArGe FAQ](https://arge-am-post.de/jungsammler/faq-am-post/)
- A 2008 guide advises getting AM Post stamps catalogued above about €5 checked (it names Hettler or Schlegel); cheaper stamps may go unchecked if cleanly cancelled. — [VPS Part 1](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-1-die-basissammlung.html)

- The ArGe has discussed whether perforation variants can be proven to come from Westermann (the Brunswick printer). — [ArGe Leseprobe 120](https://arge-am-post.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Leseprobe-120.pdf), via search summary
- Forgeries by Thobe exist of the 8 Pf, 80 Pf and 1 M (not the values here). — [Klaseboer](https://evertklaseboer.github.io/catalogue/vol1/html/bizone.htm)

### Inferences
- **How to read a gauge reading.** Michel writes perforations as horizontal : vertical, meaning top and bottom edges first, then left and right sides. So 11:11½ means 11 on top and bottom and 11½ on the sides. Brunswick perforation C must then be 11½:11, by elimination from the four gauges listed.
- **Checking with a ruler:**
  - Measure the design frame, not the paper; paper size varies with perforation placement.
  - A Washington copy should be clearly wider than a Brunswick copy of the same value, by the roughly 4 mm VPS cites.
  - The safest method is a side-by-side comparison with a cheap known Washington copy and a cheap known Brunswick copy. My sources give no absolute mm dimensions.
- **Checking the paper:**
  - Under a 10× loupe, Washington z paper shows rough, greyish-brown fibres or "wood splinters".
  - With a micrometer, Washington paper measures about 0.10–0.12 mm and Brunswick about 0.07–0.095 mm.
  - This only works off paper. The 4 Pf is glued down and would need soaking first.
- **The owner's three copies:**
  - **3 Pf:** looks dull brownish-lilac, not the blue-violet of 17 b, and is badly toned. The most valuable Brunswick combinations (17 b C/D) are therefore unlikely. A London 10 A y/G remains possible only if it gauges about 14. Toning would cut any premium sharply.
  - **4 Pf and 10 Pf:** roughly 13–14 teeth are visible along each long side, which fits gauge 11–11½ and points away from London. That is low-confidence, from photos. Their value depends entirely on the gauge on each side.

- I found no mm dimensions for the design frames of each printing. The "4 mm wider" figure comes from a 2008 club article and should be checked against a reference copy.
- The full letter key for the London perforation combinations A–G (apart from G = 14¾:14½) was not found.
- I found no **used** retail price for 22 D other than the €1,197.60 eBay asking price, and no Michel value.

### Cited Findings

- **When expertising is worthwhile.** A club guide recommends BPP checking only for AM Post stamps catalogued above about €5. — [VPS Part 1](https://vps-1894.de/am-post-teil-1-die-basissammlung.html)

### Inferences

#### Flagged — worth a closer look (step-by-step)
Tools needed: a perforation gauge (any "Instanta"-type gauge), a 10× loupe, a mm ruler, daylight or a daylight lamp, and optionally a UV lamp and a micrometer. Use stamp tongs, and do not rub or clean the stamps.

**1. AM Post 10 Pf, brown (photo 3, loose). Highest upside.**
1. Lay the stamp on the gauge and read each of the four edges separately.
2. Any side at 14 or above means it is not genuine as a London print, because London never printed a 10 Pf. Treat it as suspicious.
3. All four sides at 11 means Washington Mi 6 or Brunswick 22 A, worth €0.90–5.
4. **All four sides at 11½ means a possible Mi 22 D**, offered at about €1,200 used with a certificate. Fewer than 100 genuine used copies are known.
5. To separate Washington from Brunswick on an all-11 stamp, look with the loupe at the upper ornament just left of the "P" of PFENNIG:
   - A **white triangle** means Brunswick.
   - No white patch means Washington.
   - Washington paper also feels thicker and stiffer (about 0.10–0.12 mm against 0.07–0.095 mm).
6. If you get 11½ all round, do not soak, press or clean the stamp. Send it to a BPP AM-Post examiner (Schlegel or Wehner) for a certificate before offering it, because fake perforations exist.

**2. AM Post 4 Pf, grey (photo 6, glued).**
1. Read the gauge. If you can't read it through the paper, soak the stamp off in cold water first and dry it flat.
2. Top and bottom 11, sides 11½ = **18 B**: about €21 signed, €3–25 asking.
3. 11½ on top and bottom only (18 C) or all round (18 D) = €4–5.
4. All 11 = 18 A (€0.50) or Washington 2 (€0.40, or €3 for x paper with a signature).
5. Gauge about 14 = London 11 C, about €2.
6. Look at the two dark specks in the oval against a light. Pinholes would lower value.

**3. AM Post 3 Pf (photo 2, loose).**
1. Read the gauge.
2. **About 14 on all sides = London (Mi 10)**: €8–35 depending on the perforation letter. Under the loupe a London stamp shows a dot screen. It then needs an expert to assign the letter and paper.
3. If it reads 11/11½, compare the colour with a known 3 Pf. Only a clearly **blue-violet** copy (17 b) with 11½ somewhere (C or D) is worth checking further.
4. The heavy toning means even a good variety would sell at a steep discount.

**4. Control Council shades: 75 Pf (934), 24 Pf (951), 15 Pf (948), and with smaller upside 16 Pf (949), 20 Pf (924) and 50 Pf numeral (932).**
1. Compare each one in daylight with a second, common copy of the same value; ordinary copies are easy to buy for cents.
2. Only a distinctly different, deeper or brighter tone is worth pursuing.
3. A colour check by the Kontrollrat working group ("ARGE farbgeprüft") or a BPP examiner is needed before any premium can be asked. Most copies turn out to be the common shade.
4. The owner should get a fee quote before sending anything; fees can exceed the upside for stamps under about €20.

**5. Not worth further effort:** 6 Pf (916), 12 Pf (920), 40 Pf (929), 2 Pf (943), 8 Pf (945), 50 Pf pictorial (955) and the Bohemia & Moravia 2.50 K (141). Their known premium varieties are worth about €2–4 at most, or exist mainly as mint or plate pieces.

### Gaps
- I found no BPP or ARGE fee schedule, so I can't say exactly where expertising stops paying.
- I could not verify the exchange rate for Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- **High asking prices with no evidence of sales at that level:**

  - 17 b D at €220–287.50 and 920 c at €390.

- **Weak demand even for "catalogue-valuable" items:** a 17 b B z plate-flaw stamp with "Kat.-Wert 350 €" was at €25 with 0 bids, the London 10 A y auction at €15 had 0 bids, and a cover with two 10 G "ca 250 €" was at €65 with 0 bids ([327457673387](https://www.ebay.de/itm/327457673387)). At the time of capture these suggest real selling prices sit well below catalogue for specialised AM Post and Kontrollrat items. That is an inference from snapshots, not completed sales.
- **Uncertified copies are cheap.** Every premium-priced listing above carries a BPP or ARGE signature, report or certificate. Uncertified copies of the same numbers, such as Mi 1 x and 6 x singles at €1.90, sell as ordinary stamps.
