## Q1. What exactly is each stamp (Michel, Scott), what is its used catalogue value, and what does it realistically sell for?

### Takeaway
All 26 stamps are mass-produced Bundespost definitives and commemoratives from 1965–1975. Most had print runs of hundreds of millions to billions. Each is catalogued at or near the catalogue minimum used: Scott's floor is US$0.25 per stamp, and Michel values are in the tens of euro cents. The realistic resale value is a few cents each, or at best about €1 as a nicely sorted single. Two pages are glued down and marked with felt-pen ink, which lowers value further. The biggest preliminary error was the stamp at the right edge of photo 7: it is the 30 Pf "Nordertor Flensburg", not a "Bundesverfassungsgericht" stamp.

### Inferences
- **Value.** These are huge printings: 30 million (Söderblom) up to 4.3 billion (851) copies. That puts every stamp at the catalogue floor. A realistic resale value is a few cents each in bulk, and perhaps €0.10–0.30 for the better-condition commemoratives (Söderblom, IVA) and the 2 DM if sold individually. I have no exact sale for those three, so these figures are my judgement from comparable data.
- **Lot total.** If every stamp were at Scott's US$0.25 floor, the 26 stamps would total about US$6.50 in catalogue value. The realistic cash value of the whole group is likely only a few euros, and less if the stamps stay glued to the pages.
- **Condition.** The stamps glued onto the brown pages (photos 6 and 7) would need soaking off before any dealer would sort them. Several have felt-pen ink on or beside them, and one Heinemann is defaced by ink across the face. Such copies are effectively unsaleable as singles.

### Gaps
- **Michel used values.** I could not obtain actual Michel used values for any number; the Michel catalogue and Michel-Online are paywalled. The only Michel-related figures I found are seller statements: €0.30 for 849 mint ([finn.no](https://www.finn.no/437157794)), and a seller's "Michel €60" for a 21-value used Heinemann set dominated by high values ([eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/203511314485)). ## Q2. Which known varieties of these exact values are worth noticeably more (above about €5), how can a non-expert check, and what are they worth?

### Takeaway
I found real, catalogued varieties for these values, but none of them is worth much. The most likely in this lot is a booklet stamp with a straight-cut top or bottom edge, or a coil stamp with a counting number on the back. A coloured (red, green or blue) counting number is the scarcest. These sell for roughly €1–5 at dealers (mint) and about €1 used on eBay. I found no documented variety of these exact values that would reliably exceed €5 as a used single. The photos show no sign of anything better: all visible edges are perforated, and both 70 Pf copies show both colours.

### Cited Findings

#### Varieties that exist for these values
- **Unfallverhütung coil stamps with counting numbers.**
  - The series was essentially a coil issue; the sheet printing was a small run for the philatelic mail-order offices because coil perforation was sometimes poor. On normal rolls **every fifth stamp has a counting number printed on the back**, and only this series had such numbers **in black and three other colours** — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfallverh%C3%BCtung_(Briefmarkenserie)).
  - A German dealer (Philmaster) has separate stock categories for black, red, green and blue numbers on Bund Unfallverhütung coils — [Philmaster black numbers](https://www.philmaster-shop.de/kategorie/339/briefmarken-deutschland/deutschlandbund/rollenmarken/unfall-schwarze-nr).
  - Dealer retail prices (Philmaster, mint, Nov 2026), single stamp with number:
    - **Black numbers:** 695 €0.50–1.00, 696 €0.50, 698 €0.50–1.00, 699 €0.50–1.00, 700 €0.50–1.50, 702 roll start €4.00, **773 single with odd number €3.00**. A complete 11-value set with black numbers costs €8.00 — [Philmaster black numbers](https://www.philmaster-shop.de/kategorie/339/briefmarken-deutschland/deutschlandbund/rollenmarken/unfall-schwarze-nr)

- **Unfallverhütung booklet stamps with a straight edge ("C" = cut at top, "D" = cut at bottom).**
  - From 1974 the booklets contained stamps perforated on only three sides, cut at the top or bottom. Wikipedia calls them the first Bundespost stamps not perforated all round. The 1974 cut booklet stamps are the 10, 20, 30 and 40 Pf — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfallverh%C3%BCtung_(Briefmarkenserie))
  - The Leuchtturm album index lists "Unfallverhütung, senkrechte Paare aus MH: 695 C/D, 696 C/D, 698 C/D, 699 C/D" (1974) — [Leuchtturm index PDF](https://www.leuchtturm.de/media/cms/files/t9829g095615einzelblattverzeichnis_ref_nr_23AZ.pdf)
  - These apply to 5 copies in this lot: 695, 696, 698 and both 699s.
- **Industrie und Technik paper types.** The first-issued values exist on **two paper types that specialists tell apart by their fluorescence**. All values were printed in intaglio on white fluorescent paper. Wikipedia cites berlin-briefmarken.de for this — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrie_und_Technik). The first values (40, 50 and 100 Pf) appeared on 15 May 1975, so the lot's 851s fall in that group — [stamp-store](https://www.stamp-store.com/catalog/postage-stamps-industry-and-technology-germany-federal-republic-of-germany-1975-50-pfennig)
- **Brandenburger Tor gum types.**
  - Some coil stamps got a special humidity-resistant gum and are valued "noticeably higher" — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburger_Tor_(Briefmarkenserie))
  - Michel's v/w split is shiny gum (v) against matt gum (w), with w claimed to be worth 1.6–3 times more. This is a forum claim, not a catalogue figure — [The Stamp Forum thread](https://thestampforum.boards.net/thread/8195/germany-definitives-brandenburger-1966-1967), seen via search excerpt; the page refused direct fetch.
  - Brandenburger Tor coil stamps with control numbers exist ("506-510R … Kontrollnummer 250") — [eBay listing title](https://www.ebay.de/itm/313064626225)
- **Bauwerke series I (456).** It was also issued in rolls ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bauwerke_aus_zw%C3%B6lf_Jahrhunderten)). I found no price data for numbered coil copies of 456.
- **Fluorescent paper.** allnumis lists the Unfallverhütung (695, 698, 701, 702) and Heinemann (636, 638, 639, 644) stamps as printed on fluorescent paper ([allnumis 702](https://www.allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/germany/miscellaneous/100-pfennig-crate-on-hoist-49382); [allnumis 639](https://www.allnumis.com/stamps-catalog/germany/people-personalities-man/40-pfennig-gustav-heinemann-1899-1976-1971-5888)). A glow under UV is normal for these.
- **Plate flaws vs. printing accidents.** A plate flaw repeats identically at the same sheet position and is catalogued and valued. One-off printing accidents have no fixed Michel value — [Briefmarkenmesse Essen glossary](https://www.briefmarkenmesse-essen.de/glossar/fehldruck/). I found no listed plate flaws with values for any of these stamps.
- **Sheet-margin pieces with printer's marks (Druckerzeichen, Formnummer).** These carry small premiums, for example a mint 698 with printer's mark DZ 7 at €3.45 asking ([PicClick/eBay](https://picclick.de/698-Unfallverh%C3%BCtung-30-Pf-DZ-7-Einzelmarke-125648450456.html)). #### Flagged — worth a closer look (step-by-step checks for a non-expert)
Expectation up front: even a positive result is worth about €1–5 per stamp, not more.

1. **Straight-edge (booklet) check** for the 10, 20, 30 and two 40 Pf Unfallverhütung (695, 696, 698, 699×2).
   - Hold each stamp with the lettering upright.
   - Look at the **top and bottom edges only**. A booklet stamp (C or D) has one of these edges cut perfectly straight, with no perforation teeth.
   - In my close-ups all visible edges show teeth. The lower edges of the photo 7 30 Pf and 40 Pf are partly hidden by a neighbouring stamp or ink, so check those two in person.
   - If found: used C/D singles trade at about €1–2 (see Q4).
2. **Coil number check** (every 5th Unfallverhütung coil stamp has a number printed on the back).
   - Check the **loose 70 Pf (photo 3)** first. Turn it over and look for a small printed number, usually black, sometimes red, green or blue.
   - The glued copies could only be checked after soaking in cold water. Caution: the felt-pen ink on the pages may run, and the realistic gain is small.
   - Worth noting if found: a 773 with number (dealer €3 mint), a red-numbered 40 or 50 Pf (€3–5 mint), a green-numbered 20 Pf, or a blue- or red-numbered 10 Pf (€2 mint). Used copies fetch about €1 on eBay.
   - The loose 20 Pf Lorsch (456) and the two Brandenburger Tor stamps also come from coil printings. Check their backs too; I found no price data for these.
3. **UV check** for the Industrie und Technik 30, 50 and 70 Pf (849, 851×2, 852).
   - In a dark room, shine a long-wave UV torch (common 365 nm type) on the stamps.
   - Compare the paper glow of the two 50 Pf copies against each other and against the 30 and 70 Pf.
   - A clearly different glow (for example dull or violet against bright) may indicate the scarcer paper type. Only a specialist can confirm it, and I found no price premium data. Treat it as curiosity-level.
4. **Missing-colour check** on the two-colour 70 Pf (773) and the multicoloured IVA 20 Pf (471).
   - Confirm every normal colour is present. For 773 that is blue design plus green "70" and lettering. Both of the lot's copies are normal.
   - A genuinely missing colour would be a major error. I found no catalogue entry for one on these stamps. Faded or chemically altered copies are common, so any apparent missing colour needs certification by a BPP expert before it is worth anything.
5. **"Berlin" check.**
   - Read the inscription on every stamp. If any says "DEUTSCHE BUNDESPOST BERLIN", it is the scarcer Berlin version (different Michel numbers; Berlin print runs were much smaller per [Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfallverh%C3%BCtung_(Briefmarkenserie))).
   - All stamps I could read in these photos say only "Deutsche Bundespost".
6. **Hidden-value Heinemann (photo 7).** Only out of curiosity: its value is under the overlapping 8 Pf stamp. Ink on the face makes it worthless in any case.

### Inferences
- **Brandenburger Tor.** The v/w gum distinction is irrelevant for these used copies. Gum is lost on used, soaked or glued stamps, so shiny and matt cannot be told apart.
- **Booklet stamps.** The used Unfallverhütung C/D stamps are listed in the catalogue but trade at about €1–2 (Q4). They would not lift any stamp above the €5 threshold.
- **Paper-fluorescence varieties.** The Industrie und Technik paper types are specialist material with no published price data I could find. Given the 4.3-billion print run of 851, a large premium is unlikely.

### Gaps
- No Michel catalogue figures (mint or used) for the C/D booklet stamps, the "R" coil-number stamps, or the Industrie und Technik paper types. Michel is paywalled, and the dealer pages I could read show mint retail prices only.
- No information found on coil-number varieties or prices for Heinemann, Industrie und Technik, Brandenburger Tor or Bauwerke 456 singles.
- No documented plate flaws with values found for any of the 21 issues. Michel-Spezial would be the source.

## Q3. Overall: does anything in this group plausibly exceed US$10 per stamp?

### Takeaway
**No.** Nothing in this group plausibly exceeds US$10 per stamp, used or as a variety. The best-case finds would be a coloured-number coil stamp or a straight-edged booklet stamp. These are listed by dealers at €2–5 even in mint condition, and used copies sell for about €1. The whole group of 26 is realistically worth a few euros at most.

### Cited Findings
- **Coil singles with numbers.** Mint singles with black numbers retail at €0.50–1.50, with a 70 Pf (773) at €3.00 — [Philmaster black numbers](https://www.philmaster-shop.de/kategorie/339/briefmarken-deutschland/deutschlandbund/rollenmarken/unfall-schwarze-nr). Red-number singles retail at €2–5 — [Philmaster red numbers](https://www.philmaster-shop.de/kategorie/340/briefmarken-deutschland/deutschlandbund/rollenmarken/unfall-rote-nr).
- **Used booklet stamp.** A used Berlin 40 Pf Unfallverhütung booklet stamp (407 C, the Berlin equivalent of 699 C) is marked sold at €1.00 — [eBay](https://www.ebay.de/itm/117447123065).
- **High asking prices are specialist lots, not singles.** A "Bund Rollenmarken spezial Unfallverhütung" lot with coloured numbers and roll-end strips is asked at €400 — [eBay.at](https://www.ebay.at/itm/365223446749).
- **Scott floor.** Scott's minimum catalogue value is US$0.25 per stamp — [Scott catalogue tutorial](https://www.scribd.com/document/430452061/ScottCatalogueTutorial).

### Inferences
- The only route to more than US$10 would be a genuine major error, such as a missing colour or an unlisted plate flaw confirmed by an expert. I saw no sign of that in the photos, and no such error is documented in the sources I found.

### Gaps
- I could not check Michel-Spezial for rare listed sub-varieties of these values. Any such variety would need expert examination, and nothing in the photos suggests one.
